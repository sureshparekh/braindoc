brainsp.line_catalogue#

BRAIN: which emission lines exist, where they are, and on which wavelength scale.

Pure data and one dispersion formula. This module imports numpy and nothing else, so the settings validator and the desktop interface can ask what lines and species exist without starting a JAX backend in the process that draws the menus. emission_lines imports from here and adds the design matrix; this is the same split reddening_catalogue makes against reddening_laws, and for the same reason.

THIS MODULE IS ABOUT THE OBSERVED SPECTRUM’S WAVELENGTH SCALE AND NOTHING ELSE. The template library’s scale is a separate open question: an SSP file states its own wavelengths, BRAIN resamples the library onto the observed grid, and nothing here inspects or converts that grid. Conflating the two would mean a single setting silently moving both the lines and the stellar continuum, which are measured against each other. Do not extend this module to the library without settling that question first.

VACUUM IS THE SOURCE OF TRUTH, AIR IS DERIVED

Every wavelength below is stored once, in vacuum, at the precision its source gives. The air set is computed from it at import time. Both sets end up module-level, importable and printable, so both are available to be inspected; only one is maintained.

Hardcoding both would drift: a label and a number written separately can come to disagree while the code around them is corrected. Deriving one set from the other removes the opportunity, because there is only one number.

THE CONVERSION

Ciddor (1996), Applied Optics 35, 1566, equation 1, for standard air: dry air at 15 C, 101325 Pa, 0 per cent humidity, 450 ppm CO2.

sigma^2 = (1e4 / lambda_vac[A])^2
n       = 1 + 5.792105e-2 / (238.0185 - sigma^2)
            + 1.67917e-3 / (57.362 - sigma^2)
lambda_air = lambda_vac / n

The inverse needs no iteration: lambda_vac = lambda_air * n(lambda_air). Evaluating n at the air wavelength instead of the vacuum one is a second order error, and the measured round trip over the catalogue’s own span, 3727 to 22477 A, closes to 0.0022 km/s. Over 2000 to 25000 A it closes to 0.014 km/s, the worst of it at the blue end where the dispersion is steepest. Both are four orders below the 83 km/s the conversion itself is worth, so the analytic inverse is used rather than a fixed point iteration.

THE 2000 A GATE

Below 2000 A the conversion returns the vacuum wavelength unchanged, and that is a physical statement, not a numerical guard.

- Air is opaque below about 2000 A: molecular oxygen absorbs, there is no
  propagating beam, and "the wavelength this line would have in air" is
  not a measurable quantity. The universal convention, NIST ASD included,
  is that wavelengths below 2000 A are quoted in vacuum and never
  converted.
- Ciddor's expression has poles. Its denominators vanish at sigma^2 =
  238.0185 and 57.362, which is 648.2 A and 1320.3 A. Ungated it fails in
  two ways below 2000 A, and the quiet failure is the dangerous one. Just
  above the 1320.3 A pole it returns n = 0.945, an index below unity that
  no transparent medium has, and a shift of +77 A in the wrong direction.
  At Lyman alpha, 1215.67 A, it returns n = 1.000177 and a shift of
  -0.2 A: a perfectly ordinary looking answer to a question that has none.

VALIDITY AT THE RED END, WHICH IS AN EXTRAPOLATION

Ciddor’s dispersion rests on the measurements of Peck & Reeder (1972), which span 230 to 1690 nm. BRAIN’s catalogue reaches 22477 A, so every entry beyond 16900 A is extrapolated. This was checked against Mathar (2007), J. Opt. A 9, 470, whose multivariate fit covers 1.3 to 2.5 micron and is therefore a measurement where Ciddor is an extrapolation. Evaluating Mathar’s 1.3-2.5 micron coefficients at 288.15 K, 101325 Pa and 0 per cent humidity against Ciddor’s standard air:

- the two differ by a flat 11.7 m/s zero point across the whole band;
- that offset varies by 0.17 m/s between 1.7 and 2.25 micron, which is
  the span BRAIN's NIR entries occupy;
- it varies by 0.18 m/s across the full 1.3-2.5 micron band.

A zero point that flat is not a failure of the extrapolation, it is a difference in the two authors’ reference states. The measured figures above are what was reproduced here. A frequently repeated explanation attributes the offset to the 370 ppm CO2 Mathar adopts against Ciddor’s 450 ppm; by the standard CO2 scaling of the refractivity, 0.534e-6 per ppm, that accounts for only -3.5 m/s and in the opposite sense, so it is not the explanation. What matters for BRAIN is the shape, not the zero point: a flat offset of 11.7 m/s is 4e-5 of the conversion’s own size, so the red end of the catalogue is safe to convert.

ABOVE 2 MICRON THE CONVENTION FLIPS BACK TO VACUUM

NIST ASD changes its default output from air to vacuum at exactly 5000 cm^-1, which is 20000 A. So the six catalogue entries beyond 2 micron – H2 20332, He I 20580, H2 21218, Brgamma 21654, H2 22230 and H2 22470 – are conventionally quoted in vacuum even by observers who quote everything bluewards of them in air. A user who asks BRAIN for “air” because their optical data are air, and who then reads a NIR line list off a paper, is very likely comparing a converted air wavelength against a published vacuum one. BRAIN converts what it is told to convert; the convention is stated here so that a 82 km/s disagreement in the K band has a written explanation.

THE CHOICE OF FORMULA IS FREE AT THIS TOLERANCE

Measured here, as velocity:

formula pair                   3700 A      6563 A      22000 A
Ciddor vs Morton (2000)        0.05 m/s    0.30 m/s     1.7 m/s
Ciddor vs Edlen (1966)         4.2 m/s     3.8 m/s      2.4 m/s

Against 82-85 km/s for the conversion itself, every published formula is the same formula. What matters is whether you convert at all. The one genuinely wrong option is the truncated series that circulates as “the SDSS formula”,

n = 1 + 2.735182e-4 + 131.4182/lam^2 + 2.76249e8/lam^4

which departs from Ciddor by 0.14 km/s at H alpha and 0.26 km/s at 22000 A. That is still small, but it is a systematic with no source behind it, and it is 30 to 60 times the spread among the real formulae.

THE NAMING RULE

The integer in a catalogue name is a CONVENTIONAL LABEL. It is not the wavelength, it is not rounded from the wavelength, and nothing may derive a wavelength from it. He I_10830 holds 10833.138. H2_21218 holds 21218.337, whose air value is 21212.547. Pabeta_12820 holds 12821.576, whose air value is 12818.070, so the label rounds to neither scale.

Every label here is the traditional one and every label stays as it is, so no consumer that matches a name breaks. This is what lets the [O II] doublet keep the literature labels 3726 and 3729 rather than being renamed to their vacuum roundings 3727 and 3730: renaming would have put one component under [O II]_3727, which is the historic name of the UNRESOLVED BLEND, and every reader of that name would have taken a single component for the sum of two.

PROVENANCE, LINE BY LINE

Riffel et al. (2019) remains the authority for WHICH lines belong in the catalogue. It is not the source of the numbers. Every atomic wavelength below is from the NIST Atomic Spectra Database, and every molecular hydrogen wavelength is from Roueff, Abgrall, Czachorowski, Pachucki, Puchalski & Komasa (2019), A&A 630, A58, Table 2, because NIST ASD carries no molecular hydrogen.

NIST entries are Ritz wavelengths – computed from the fitted energy levels – except where marked. Ritz values are usually the more precise of the two because they are constrained by the whole level system rather than by one measurement.

FOUR DELIBERATE CHOICES, EACH MEASURED

  1. [S II] 6716 and 6731 use NIST OBSERVED rather than Ritz. The two agree

inside their stated uncertainties, so this is not a claim that one is
more accurate. It is a claim about continuity: the observed values
reduce through Ciddor to 6716.440 and 6730.815 in air, which are the
canonical numbers every published [S II] density calibration is keyed
to, while the Ritz values give 6716.43 and 6730.80. BRAIN's own density
diagnostic reads this doublet, and the calibration it reads is quoted
against the canonical pair.

  1. He I 5876 uses the emissivity-weighted centroid of the 1s2p 3P* - 1s3d

3D multiplet, 5877.289, which sits 0.046 A -- 2.35 km/s -- redward of
the strongest single component, 5877.243. BRAIN fits this line as one
Gaussian against a blend it cannot resolve, and the centre of a single
Gaussian fitted to that blend is the emissivity centroid, not the
strongest component.

  1. [S III] 9069 and 9531 are given to one decimal because that is all

there is. The S III 1D2 upper level is tabulated in NIST ASD to 0.1
cm^-1, which is 0.08 A at 9069 A, so a third decimal here would be
invented. The air values that follow, 9068.61 and 9530.59, carry the
same 0.08 A uncertainty.

  1. [P II] 11470 and 11886 are Ritz values computed here from the NIST ASD

LEVELS tables, because ASD has no P II line entries in this range at
all. They are therefore one derivation removed from the other atomic
entries, and are given to one decimal for the same reason as [S III].

THE FIVE ENTRIES THAT WERE UNDER REVIEW, AND WHAT THE REVIEW FOUND

Five NIR entries were held at the whole-Angstrom value they had always had, because each was reported MISIDENTIFIED – the wrong species or the wrong transition, not merely a rounded wavelength – and a precise wavelength for the wrong transition is worse than a rounded one, because it looks authoritative. The review is done. Every value below was re-derived here from the NIST ASD LEVELS tables as 1e8/d(sigma) and agreed with the published Ritz wavelength to the last printed digit.

Three of the five claims were upheld, two were not:

He II_10122     IDENTIFICATION UPHELD, WAVELENGTH WRONG. It is He II,
                not the Pa gamma or He I blend that was alleged: Pa gamma
                is 820 A away, and NIST lists no He I transition between
                10074.786 and 10141.203, so there is nothing to blend
                with. The line is the n = 5 -> 4 multiplet, 19 components
                spanning 10125.628 to 10126.762, whose g.A-weighted
                centroid is 10126.385 -- the same construction as He II
                4686. The old 10122.0 was 129.8 km/s blueward of that.

[Fe II]_12950   SPECIES UPHELD, MULTIPLET CLAIM REJECTED. It was alleged
                to be the wrong member of the multiplet. It is not: the
                only other forbidden [Fe II] line in the window is
                a6D1/2 - a4D3/2 at 12981.246, 720 km/s away. The entry is
                a6D5/2 - a4D5/2 and always was; only the number was
                wrong, by +87.9 km/s. 12946.2030, Ritz, +-0.0010 A.

[Fe II]_15342   SPECIES UPHELD, "NOT [Fe II]" REJECTED. It is the
                forbidden a4F9/2 - a4D5/2 line at 15338.9393, Ritz,
                +-0.0016 A; the old value was +59.8 km/s redward. There
                are permitted Fe II components at 15340.54 and 15343.04,
                but they are high-excitation 3d6(5D)6p-6d transitions,
                not nebular, and no NIR line list identifies the 1.534
                micron feature as anything but the forbidden line. That
                exclusion rests on the physics; no paper was found that
                states it outright.

[Fe II]+Br11    BLEND CLAIM UPHELD. [Fe II] a4F7/2 - a4D5/2 is at
                16773.4056 and Br 11-4 at 16811.129: 37.7 A apart, which
                is 674 km/s. Resolving that needs only R > 450, and every
                NIR spectrograph that reaches 1.68 micron does far
                better; Riffel et al. 2006, the source of this entry's
                label, tabulates the two as separate rows with
                independent fluxes. Worse, Br 11 was named in the label
                and then never modelled -- the composite carried one
                column at 16773, so 16811 was left to the continuum. The
                entry is now [Fe II]_16773, and Br11_16811 is a line in
                its own right with a column of its own.

H2_20580        MISIDENTIFICATION UPHELD. It is He I 1s2s 1S0 - 1s2p
                1P*1 at 20586.90463, Ritz, +-0.00003 A, and Riffel et al.
                2006 labels this row He I, not H2. The nearest H2
                transition observable in a galaxy spectrum -- v_upper <=
                3 -- is 3-2 S(5) at 20655.84, 1104 km/s away; the nearest
                member of the 1-0 S branch that supplies the catalogue's
                other H2 entries is 1-0 S(2) at 20337.578, 242 A away.
                The entry is now He I_20580, 100.6 km/s redward of the
                value it replaces.

Both species corrections keep the line in kinematic group 0, so nothing moves between groups, and Br11 joins group 0 with the rest of the series.

WHY THIS MATTERED, AND WHAT IT DID NOT MATTER FOR

It was separately alleged that the NIR table mixes air and vacuum. It does not. Twenty-two of the 27 entries were on one scale, vacuum, and 20 of those reproduce NIST or Roueff to 0.00 km/s. The five above were wrong by the SAME velocity in air mode as in vacuum mode, because the stored value and the truth convert through Ciddor alike and the fractional error survives. So declaring a wavelength_scale did put NIR data on one scale; what it could not do was put these five at the right place on it.

What is genuinely mixed is the integer LABELS, which are transcribed from the lambda_lab column of Riffel et al. 2006 and inherit its own mixture: mostly air (9069, 9531, 10830, 21213) but vacuum for 11886, 12570, 13209, 16773. Comparing a label against an air value therefore shows the 82 km/s air-vacuum difference for every CORRECT entry, which is almost certainly where the mixed-scale report came from. Under THE NAMING RULE above this is inert: nothing in the tree derives a wavelength from a name, and a sweep for digit-matching against line names found no such code. The labels stay.

The cost of the five bad numbers was not in the conversion but in the kinematic groups. [Fe II], H2 and He I all sit in group 0 and share one velocity, and the six [Fe II] entries carried errors of 0.0, +87.9, 0.0, +59.8, 0.0 and -7.2 km/s – a 95 km/s spread inside a single shared- velocity group, with the old H2_20580 a further -100.6 km/s from the four H2 lines it was tied to. For scale, the optical defect corrected in the commit before this one was a 28.8 km/s spread between H alpha and [N II] 6583, and it was enough to produce near-maximal antisymmetric residuals, 45 spurious 3-sigma outliers and twice the whole-spectrum chi2.

That damage is argued, not measured: no NIR spectrum ships with this repository, so none of these entries has been exercised on data. The optical half of the catalogue is what the measurements in the previous commit cover.

One boundary, two numbers, because the boundary is a wavelength and the two scales disagree by 97 km/s this far into the blue. air_to_vacuum gates here and vacuum_to_air gates at AIR_CONVERSION_FLOOR, so the pair is an identity everywhere rather than everywhere except the 0.65 A between the two expressions of the floor, where a vacuum wavelength would have converted down across the boundary and then refused to come back.

brainsp.line_catalogue.refractive_index_of_air(wavelength)[source]#

The refractive index of standard air at a VACUUM wavelength in Angstrom.

Ciddor (1996) equation 1: dry air at 15 C, 101325 Pa, 0 per cent humidity, 450 ppm CO2.

Returns exactly 1.0 below AIR_CONVERSION_FLOOR, so that a caller who multiplies or divides by this index gets the wavelength back unchanged. Below 2000 A air does not transmit, the air wavelength is not a physical quantity, and the formula itself has poles at 1320.3 A and 648.2 A where its denominators vanish. Ungated it fails in two ways there, and the quiet one is the dangerous one: at 1320 A it returns 0.945, an index below unity that no transparent medium has, while at Lyman alpha it returns 1.000177 and a shift of 0.2 A, which looks like an answer and is not one.

brainsp.line_catalogue.vacuum_to_air(wavelength_vacuum)[source]#

Vacuum wavelength to standard-air wavelength, in Angstrom.

The shift is 82 to 85 km/s across BRAIN’s catalogue: 85.2 km/s at [O II] 3726, 82.8 at H alpha, 81.8 at the reddest H2 entry. It is very nearly constant in velocity, which is exactly what makes getting the scale wrong so hard to see in a fit.

Below AIR_CONVERSION_FLOOR the input is returned unchanged. See refractive_index_of_air.

brainsp.line_catalogue.air_to_vacuum(wavelength_air)[source]#

Standard-air wavelength to vacuum wavelength, in Angstrom.

lambda_vac = lambda_air * n(lambda_air), with no iteration. Evaluating the index at the air wavelength rather than at the vacuum one it is defined against is a second order error: the measured round trip vacuum -> air -> vacuum closes to 0.0022 km/s over the catalogue’s own 3727-22477 A span and to 0.014 km/s over 2000-25000 A. A fixed point iteration would buy nothing measurable and would put a loop in a path that does not need one.

Below AIR_CONVERSION_FLOOR_ON_AIR_SCALE the input is returned unchanged. That is the 2000 A floor expressed on the air scale, 1999.3526 A, rather than 2000 A again: the floor is one boundary, and gating both directions at the same number would leave the 0.65 A between the two expressions of it converting one way and not the other.

brainsp.line_catalogue.require_scale(scale)[source]#

Check a wavelength scale and return it, or raise saying what is allowed.

Refuses None as loudly as it refuses a misspelling. A scale that defaults to one of its two values is a scale that gets guessed, and the whole point of the setting is that BRAIN does not guess it; see brain_settings for the consequence of guessing wrong.

The hydrogen recombination lines, in vacuum Angstrom, keyed by series name.

NIST ASD Ritz values throughout: n -> 2 for the Balmer series, n -> 3 for Paschen, n -> 4 for Brackett.

This table exists separately from the emission-line catalogue because the Case B predictions in atomic_data cover hydrogen lines the catalogue does not fit – H11, H12 – and the catalogue carries lines that are not hydrogen. Where the two overlap, the catalogue entry is written as a reference to this dict rather than as a second literal, so there is one number per transition and it cannot be edited in one place and not the other.

brainsp.line_catalogue.HYDROGEN_SERIES_EXTRA_VACUUM = {'H11': 3771.7011, 'H12': 3751.2174}#

Balmer lines predicted by the Case B tables but not fitted by BRAIN.

NIST ASD Ritz, n = 11 and 12 -> 2. They are separate from HYDROGEN_LINES_VACUUM only so that a reader of that dict sees exactly the transitions the catalogue can fit; atomic_data takes the union.

brainsp.line_catalogue.ENTRIES_UNDER_REVIEW = ()#

Catalogue entries whose IDENTIFICATION, not whose precision, is in doubt.

Empty, and kept rather than deleted so that a consumer which asks keeps getting an answer. It held five NIR entries until the review recorded in the module docstring resolved all five: three kept their species and gained a correct wavelength, [Fe II]+Br11_16773 split into two lines, and H2_20580 became He I_20580. Nothing is outstanding.

An entry belongs here when the species or the transition is disputed, not when the wavelength is merely imprecise – imprecision is recorded in the provenance notes instead. The distinction matters because a line under review must not be given a full-precision value: a precise wavelength for the wrong transition looks authoritative and is harder to catch than a rounded one.

brainsp.line_catalogue.emission_lines_on_scale(scale)[source]#

The whole catalogue, {name: rest wavelength}, on a declared scale.

Returns a copy, because a caller that filters or edits the result must not be able to reach back into the module-level table. The scale is required and unknown values raise; see require_scale.

brainsp.line_catalogue.hydrogen_lines_on_scale(scale)[source]#

The species every catalogue entry and every tied doublet belongs to.

Declared, never parsed from the name. Kinematic group membership, line flux extraction and the Case B comparison all look their answer up here, so a new catalogue entry without an entry here is invisible to all three rather than being quietly assigned by string matching. Kinematic groups were once assigned with name.startswith(prefix), and ‘He I’ is a prefix of ‘He II’, which put the highest-ionisation line in the optical catalogue in the low-ionisation group.

The two kinematic groups BRAIN fits by default: low ionisation and high.

Group 1 holds the species whose ionisation potentials put them in a different part of the gas. Every other species shares one velocity and one dispersion.

The high-order Balmer lines H8, H9 and H10 sit in group 0 with the rest of the series. Hepsilon was already listed here before any of the four had a catalogue entry behind it, so the list named a species BRAIN could not fit; adding the lines is what makes all four live.

brainsp.line_catalogue.species_of(name)[source]#

The species a catalogue entry belongs to, declared rather than parsed.

Returns None for an entry that is not in LINE_SPECIES, so a caller can tell “no species recorded” from a species that happens to be falsy. A user-supplied line that omits one is a validation failure, not something to guess at from its name.
