brainsp.ifu_output#

BRAIN — the megacube’s extensions, and the compatibility contract.

A megacube is the cube that was fitted with the fit appended to it as extra extensions. The names, the mixed-case spelling, the DATA<n> cards inside PoPBins and the linear wavelength axis on FLXOBS follow the published MaNGA megacubes of Riffel et al. 2023 (MNRAS 524, 5640), because tooling written against those files reads extensions by name and takes its wavelength axis from FLXOBS’s CRVAL3 and CD3_3 with no fallback. A BRAIN megacube that spells one of them differently, or leaves the WCS off, does not open at all.

Two consequences run through this module. Extension names are set as header cards rather than through astropy’s name=, which upper-cases them. And FLXOBS and FLXSYN are written on a linear grid – the only place BRAIN resamples an observed flux, for the output copy alone and never for the fit – while BRAIN’s own spectral extensions stay on the grid the cube arrived on, where no resolution is thrown away.

The map quantities themselves are computed from the same per-template light and mass numbers io_utils.population_statistics gives the summary file, so a map value and that spaxel’s summary line are the same arithmetic.

brainsp.ifu_output.named(hdu, extname, summary='')[source]#

Give an HDU a name with its capitalisation intact.

astropy’s name= argument upper-cases what it is given, so an HDU built that way is called POPBINS and not PoPBins. Lookup by name is case-insensitive and would not notice, but anything that compares the string – a listing, a filter over extension names, a reader written against the published megacubes – would.

brainsp.ifu_output.linear_wavelength_grid(wavelength, step=1.0)[source]#

The linear grid the observed and synthetic spectra are written on.

It starts at the first whole multiple of the step inside the fitted range and ends at the last one, so that CRVAL3 is a round number and every point of the grid is covered by data. The step defaults to 1 A, which is what the published megacubes use.

brainsp.ifu_output.wcs_cards(hdu, grid)[source]#

The three cards megacube viewers build their wavelength axis from.

brainsp.ifu_output.resample_batch(wavelength, spectra, grid)[source]#

A batch of spectra moved onto another wavelength grid, flux-conserving.

The same integral the fit itself uses to reach its log grid, not a linear interpolation: between grids of unequal spacing the two are not approximations of each other, and on this repository’s reference spectrum interpolation was wrong by 18.9 per cent at its worst pixel.

JAX is imported here rather than at the top of the module so that naming an extension, reading a set of age bins or validating a pipeline costs nothing: the runner and the desktop interface both do all three long before any fitting starts, and importing JAX to do them would add seconds to every check.

brainsp.ifu_output.age_bin_membership(ages_yr, bins, is_ssp=None)[source]#

Which templates belong to each age bin, as a boolean (n_bin, n_ssp).

The convention is the published one: min exclusive, max inclusive, min < age <= max. It matters, because the standard bin sets put edges exactly on template ages, and a >= on the low edge would count such a template in two bins at once.

A template that is not a stellar population – the power law, when include_power_law is on – is never a member of any bin. Its light belongs under its own card, and adding it to a population bin would report accretion-disc light as stars.

brainsp.ifu_output.binned(percentages, membership)[source]#

Per-bin sums of a (n_spec, n_ssp) percentage array, as (n_bin, n_spec).

brainsp.ifu_output.featureless_parameter(short_name)[source]#

What a featureless component’s name says it is: (“FC”, exponent) for a power law F_nu ~ nu^-alpha, (“BB”, temperature in K) for a blackbody, or (None, None).

FC0.25 and FC1.50 give the exponent as written; the shipped library’s Power_150 writes it in hundredths; BB800 is an 800 K blackbody.

brainsp.ifu_output.distance_factor(distance_mpc)[source]#

4 pi d^2 / L_sun: an apparent mass (flux / template flux per Msun) times this, and times the flux unit, is a mass in solar masses.

brainsp.ifu_output.flux_unit_scale(bunit)[source]#

The number a flux unit carries: 1e-17 for MaNGA’s ‘1E-17 erg/s/cm^2/ Angstrom/spaxel’, 1 for a JWST cube’s ‘erg/s/cm^2/Angstrom’.

brainsp.ifu_output.unbinned_percent(percentages, membership, is_ssp=None)[source]#

How much of each spaxel’s light or mass no age bin claims.

Age bins as published have gaps between them, and a template library whose ages differ from the one the bins were drawn for can drop a lot of light into those gaps. That is not visible in the maps themselves – every bin looks reasonable – so the number is computed here and reported.

brainsp.ifu_output.mean_log_age(percentages, ages_yr, is_ssp=None, over_all=False)[source]#

The weighted mean of log10(age), which is not the log of the mean age.

Published maps report <log age>, so a mean age computed the other way is a different quantity that looks like the same one. Templates with a non-positive age – a power law has no age – carry no term.

over_all chooses the normalisation. False divides by the weight of the stellar templates alone, the mean age of the stars. True divides by the weight of every component, featureless ones included, which is how the megacubes of Riffel et al. (2023) define Mage_L: there 10 per cent of AGN light lowers the map by about 0.94 dex.

brainsp.ifu_output.mean_metallicity(percentages, metallicities, is_ssp=None, over_all=False)[source]#

The weighted mean of Z as a mass fraction, not of [Z/H].

Linear Z is what the published maps carry, and the two differ by more than a constant because the average of a logarithm is not the logarithm of the average. over_all as in mean_log_age.

brainsp.ifu_output.signal_to_noise(flux, inverse_sigma, window)[source]#

S/N per pixel averaged over the normalisation window.

flux and inverse_sigma are the fit’s own arrays, so this is the signal-to-noise the fitter sees rather than a second estimate of it made from the file. Pixels the fit excluded carry inverse_sigma 0 and are left out; a spaxel with no usable pixel in the window reports 0.

brainsp.ifu_output.text_value(value, comment)[source]#

A string card value cut so that the card keeps its comment.

A FITS card is eighty columns and holds the keyword, the value and the comment; a value cut to what fits the value field alone leaves the comment to be truncated instead, which is how a card ends up saying “see the BRAIN IFU documentati”. The value is the part that varies, so it is the part that gives way.

brainsp.ifu_output.pop_bins_hdu(planes, shape, positions, unfitted=None)[source]#

The map stack, as one 3D extension addressed by DATA<n> cards.

planes is an ordered sequence of (name, comment, values), where values is one number per fitted spaxel; shape is (n_y, n_x) and positions is the (y, x) of each fitted spaxel.

A spaxel that was not fitted is 0, which is what the published megacubes carry and what the viewers written against them scale their colour bars to – NaN limits break those viewers outright. Since 0 is also a plausible value for several of these quantities, the stack carries a BadSpaxel plane of its own, so that the question “is this spaxel a measurement” can be answered inside the same extension as the maps, and not only from BADSPXL and STATUS.

That plane is the one place where 0 is the wrong fill, and unfitted names the planes whose background is something else: a spaxel nobody fitted is a bad spaxel, so its BadSpaxel is 1 and not the 0 that would otherwise be left there by never writing to it. Getting this wrong is invisible – the plane exists, it is full of zeros, and every unfitted spaxel reads as a converged fit.

The card value is the quantity’s name and the card’s comment describes it, which is the convention the analysis tools index the stack by.

brainsp.ifu_output.power_law_card(short_name)[source]#

The card a non-stellar component is reported under.

The published cubes call an F_nu ~ nu^-1.5 power law FC1.50, and the shipped library calls the same component Power_150, so a power law named that way has its exponent read out of the name. Every other name is used unchanged: a blackbody BB800 is not a power law of exponent 8.00, and reading the digits out of it filed hot dust under FC. Either way the component is reported on its own card and never inside a population bin.

brainsp.ifu_output.trust_mask(values, sn_keep=None, converged=None)[source]#

Why each spaxel of a map is or is not a measurement, as a bit field.

0 is a spaxel worth reading and every other value names a reason it is not: the chosen signal-to-noise threshold threw it away, its fit did not converge, or the plane carries nothing finite there. The reasons are bits rather than an enumeration because a spaxel can fail all three at once and a map that reported only the first would understate the damage.

This is the layer the maps need and did not have. A megacube writes 0 into a spaxel nobody fitted, and 0 is a plausible value for most of these quantities, so a map without a companion mask cannot be read safely by anything – including a person.

brainsp.ifu_output.trusted_values(values, mask)[source]#

The map with every untrustworthy spaxel blanked, as NaN.

brainsp.ifu_output.spatial_wcs(header)[source]#

The celestial part of a cube’s world coordinate system, as plain cards.

Only the two spatial axes are carried over: the third axis of a cube is wavelength and a two-dimensional map that kept CRVAL3 would claim an axis it does not have. A header with no celestial solution yields nothing, which is the honest answer and is what the exported file then says.

brainsp.ifu_output.plane_hdus(values, mask, *, plane, cube, units='', wcs=None, settings=None)[source]#

One map plane as a FITS file: the values, and the mask that qualifies them.

The primary array is the map as the interface draws it, which means NaN wherever the mask says the spaxel is not a measurement, so a reader that does nothing but imshow the primary sees what the interface saw. The TRUST extension carries the reason bits, so a reader that wants the excluded spaxels back knows exactly which they are and why each one went.

The header records which plane, which cube and which settings produced the file, because a map in isolation is a picture of nothing: the same array means different things under a different signal-to-noise cut, and a file that does not say which cut it was written under cannot be checked against the cube it came from.

brainsp.ifu_output.write_plane_fits(path, values, mask, **cards)[source]#

brainsp.ifu_output.map_figure(values, mask, *, plane, cube, units='', colour_map='viridis', vmin=None, vmax=None, scale='', cut='', orientation='', figsize=(5.0, 4.4))[source]#

One map as a figure meant for print rather than for the screen.

White ground, black text and a stated unit on every axis, because the interface’s dark palette is a property of the interface and a figure dropped into a paper is read on paper. The colour maps on offer are all perceptually uniform, and the spaxels that are not measurements are grey AND hatched AND counted in the caption, so none of the three states is carried by colour alone.

Nothing is drawn that is not data or the scale it is read against: no grid, no box around the legend, no frame around the figure.
