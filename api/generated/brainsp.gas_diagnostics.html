brainsp.gas_diagnostics#

BRAIN — nebular diagnostics measured from the fitted emission lines.

Two things BRAIN measures per spectrum that were previously taken from config:

1. Electron density from the [S II] 6716/6731 doublet ratio, and electron
   temperature from the [O III] (4959+5007)/4363 ratio. BRAIN measures both
   on the fly and only falls back to standard conditions (100 cm^-3, 10^4 K)
   when the lines are too weak to be trusted.

2. Spectral classification on the BPT diagram. This is REPORTED, not acted
   on. It is a diagnostic, and a continuum-model-dependent one: Hbeta sits
   in deep stellar absorption, so its measured flux depends on the continuum
   model, and both BPT ratios use it. The same spectrum and the same library
   can change class when only the nebular treatment changes.

Both are guarded: a diagnostic that cannot be measured reliably returns the fallback and says so, rather than returning a confident wrong number.

What gates the nebular work is Hbeta DETECTABILITY, not BPT class. If Hbeta cannot be measured then the Balmer decrement cannot be formed, so the nebular extinction and everything derived from it must not be attempted. That gate lives in brainv1 where the line fluxes are, and it is applied once. An earlier version had a class-based downgrade here AND a Balmer-detection guard in brainv1 that silently overrode it, so photopop mode ran on some spectra and not others for reasons that had nothing to do with either rule.

Density from the [S II] doublet, via the Proxauf, Ottl & Kimeswenger (2014) calibration.

Returns (n_e, ok). ok is False when the lines are too weak or the ratio sits outside the density-sensitive window, in which case n_e is the default rather than an extrapolation.

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.electron_temperature(lines, ne=100.0)[source]#

Temperature from the [O III] auroral-to-nebular ratio (Osterbrock & Ferland 2006, Eq. 5.4), solved iteratively because the collisional de-excitation correction itself depends on T_e.

THE NUMERATOR IS THE NEBULAR SUM, F(4959) + F(5007), and a fitted doublet weight is not that. The [O III] column is built with its components’ ratios relative to 5007, so its weight is F(5007) alone, which is what the BPT diagram wants and what the line table reports. doublet_nebular_sum_factor supplies the missing F(4959), at the branching ratio the design matrix itself used, so the diagnostic and the model cannot drift apart.

The error this repairs was one-sided. Using F(5007) where F(4959) + F(5007) belongs makes R low by a factor 1.33510, and T_e = 3.29e4 / ln(R corr / 7.90) RISES as R falls, so every measured temperature came out too hot, and by more the hotter the gas: a gas at 10^4 K was reported at 10963 K and one at 2 x 10^4 K at 24263 K, high by 9.6 and 21.3 per cent of the right answer. It does not stop at a reported number. measure_conditions feeds T_e back into the density, and brainv1 rebuilds the NebularModel and refits whenever the measured conditions move by more than the tolerance there, so in nepop and photopop this sets the nebular continuum itself.

The R > 8 gate reads as a floor on a nebular-sum ratio now, which is how Eq. 5.4 is written; R is 1.33510 times larger than before, so ratios between 5.99 and 8.00 in the old units pass it that did not. Nothing is reported from that, because R that small implies T_e above 10^5 K and the 5000-30000 K window below rejects it. The window is where the correction changes an answer: a spectrum whose old T_e fell between 30000 and 40735 K was discarded as unphysically hot and is now measured, and one whose old T_e fell between 5000 and 5230 K was measured and is now discarded as too cold.

[O III] 4363 is intrinsically faint, so this fails far more often than it succeeds. Returns (T_e, ok).

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.classify_bpt(lines, min_snr_flux=1e-06)[source]#

Classify on the [O III]/Hb vs [N II]/Ha diagram.

Demarcations:

Kauffmann et al. (2003)  pure star-forming
Kewley et al. (2001)     maximum starburst
Schawinski et al. (2007) Seyfert / LINER split above Kewley

Returns (label, info) with label one of ‘SF’, ‘composite’, ‘Seyfert’, ‘LINER’, ‘undetermined’.

The label is a report, never a gate. It depends on the continuum model through Hbeta, which both ratios use and which sits in deep stellar absorption, so changing only the nebular treatment can change the class of an unchanged spectrum.

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.BPT_FLAG = {'LINER': 2, 'SF': 0, 'Seyfert': 3, 'composite': 1, 'undetermined': -999}#

Integer code for the BPT label, written to the summary file.

One code for each of the five classes (Not defined, Pure SF, SFComposite, LINER, Seyfert); -999 is “not defined”.

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.nebular_constrained(wavelength, inv_sigma, flux, min_snr=2.0, min_pixels=20, verbose=True)[source]#

Check whether the fit can actually distinguish a nebular continuum.

The nebular continuum is smooth and rises to the blue, which makes it nearly degenerate with dust. What breaks the degeneracy is the Balmer discontinuity at 3646 A: recombination to n=2 emits only at shorter wavelengths, so the nebular continuum is elevated blueward of the jump and drops sharply across it — a feature no reddening law or stellar template can mimic.

If that region is outside the fitted range, or too noisy to measure, then a freely fitted nebular component is unconstrained and will silently trade against A_V. Measured on the reference spectrum: S/N ~ 0.5 below the jump against ~5.5 in the red, and the fit duly ran A_V to its lower bound.

Returns (ok, info).

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.gas_kinematics_report(group_labels, group_kinematics, line_names, line_group_positions, line_fluxes, line_wavelengths, lower_bound, upper_bound, lsf_info=None)[source]#

Turn each kinematic group’s fitted sigma and v0 into something quotable, or say why it is not.

The stellar side has refused to quote a bound since the line-spread function work: instrumental_lsf.dispersion_report writes ‘<47.55’ with a leading ‘<’ and the word UPPER LIMIT rather than a number a reader could mistake for a measurement, and writes ‘unknown’ when the instrument is not known. The gas side had nothing, and a sigma_gas sitting exactly on its lower bound printed as a plain number. This is that same refusal, for the gas, and it deliberately reads the same way; on_bound is imported rather than copied so the two cannot come to disagree about what a rail is.

A group’s dispersion is not quotable for either of two reasons.

ON A BOUND

sigma_gas came back on an end of gas_dispersion_range. On the lower end the fit wanted a narrower line than it was allowed, so the value is an UPPER LIMIT and is written ‘<’; on the upper end it wanted a broader one, so it is a LOWER LIMIT and is written ‘>’. Neither is a width. This is not hypothetical: until the pixel-width floor was removed, both groups of the reference spectrum sat on 58.39 km/s and printed as measurements.

NO LINE DETECTED

The kinematics of a group are carried entirely by the lines in it. The non-negative solve puts a line at EXACTLY zero when no non-negative amplitude improves the fit, which is a statement and not a rounding, and a group whose lines all come back at zero has a flat likelihood in both sigma and v0: the optimiser returns whatever it started from or wandered to, converged, with nothing to say so. On the reference spectrum two of the high-ionisation group’s four lines ([Ne III] 3869 and the [O III] doublet) fit at exactly zero, so that group’s kinematics rest on two lines at most; a spectrum where all four go to zero would report two numbers that mean nothing at all. The count of lines carrying flux is therefore reported alongside every group, and a group with none is refused outright, v0 as well as sigma.

WHAT THE NUMBER IS A DISPERSION OF

sigma_gas is OBSERVED. The instrumental line-spread function is still in it; nothing is deconvolved from the emission line, which is why the label written beside it states the convention rather than leaving it to be assumed. sigma_star in the same summary is INTRINSIC, the instrument taken out in quadrature, so the two numbers are not comparable as printed and the file has to say which is which.

sigma_inst is reported next to sigma_gas, per group, whenever ‘instrumental_resolution’ is set, and ‘unknown’ when it is not. That follows Westfall et al. (2019), who report the observed and the instrumental dispersion as separate maps and deliberately do not do the subtraction (Section 7.3: “we do not provide the corrected velocity dispersion … We leave it to the user”). Nothing here is clipped, floored or called zero when sigma_gas comes out below sigma_inst, which over the MaNGA line-spread function of 68.0-83.5 km/s is where every sigma_gas BRAIN currently reports lands: Westfall Section 7.6.3 is explicit that such a measurement is neither equivalent to nor a valid measurement of sigma = 0, and recommends carrying the negative sigma^2 rather than clipping it. Both numbers are given so that the reader can do that arithmetic, and neither is done for them.

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.gas_dispersion_text(entry)[source]#

The token a summary or a log line prints in place of a gas dispersion.

A quotable dispersion is the number. A dispersion on the lower bound is written ‘<value’, on the upper bound ‘>value’, and a group with no detected line prints the word ‘unconstrained’ and no number at all – exactly as instrumental_lsf writes ‘<47.55’ and ‘unknown’ for the stellar side, so that the two blocks of one summary file read alike. The value the optimiser returned for an unconstrained group is not lost; it is in the reason text, where it cannot be mistaken for a result.

brainsp.gas_diagnostics.gas_velocity_text(entry)[source]#

The same, for the group’s velocity.

A velocity is not bounded in the way a dispersion is – a fit that reaches the end of gas_velocity_range has a different problem, and v0_star already reports its own – so the only refusal here is the group with no detected line, whose velocity is as unconstrained as its width.
