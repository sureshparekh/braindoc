brainsp.atomic_data#

BRAIN-v1 Atomic Data Module Reference tables for nebular physics: recombination coefficients, Balmer line ratios, Q(H) lookup tables, metal line calibrations.

All data from Osterbrock & Ferland (2006), Hummer & Storey (1987), Storey & Hummer (1995), Schaerer (2003), Leitherer et al. (1999).

brainsp.atomic_data.LAMBDA_HEPSILON = 3971.202#

The Balmer wavelengths this module needs as scalars, in VACUUM Angstrom.

Read from line_catalogue rather than written out again here. There were three tables of hydrogen wavelengths in this tree: air values to two decimals here, whole-Angstrom labels in the emission-line catalogue, and whole-Angstrom values again in METAL_LINE_CALIBRATIONS. The predicted Case B lines were therefore placed on a different scale from the lines they are printed beside and compared against. A second copy of a wavelength is a second thing to keep in step; BRAIN has already been bitten by four copies of the [N II] branching ratio.

These five are used for exactly two things: evaluating the extinction curve at H alpha and H beta, and computing NU_HALPHA and NU_HBETA for the Case B line energies. Neither is sensitive to the scale. k(lambda) for CCM changes by 2.8e-4 between the air and vacuum H alpha, which multiplies A_V in a magnitude exponent, and the photon energy changes by 2.8e-4 relative. The tables that DO care which scale they are on – the ones whose wavelengths are compared against fitted lines, or printed beside them – take the scale as an argument and do not read these constants.

LAMBDA_LYMAN_ALPHA stays a literal because it is below the 2000 A floor where an air wavelength does not exist, so vacuum is the only scale it has.

brainsp.atomic_data.hydrogen_case_b_ratios(Te)[source]#

Case B hydrogen intensities relative to H beta, with no wavelengths.

{species: ratio}, Balmer and Paschen together, interpolated in log Te and clipped to the ends of the tabulated grid.

Separate from balmer_ratios and all_hydrogen_ratios because a caller that wants only the intrinsic Balmer decrement – which is what the nebular extinction is derived from – has no wavelength to place and therefore no wavelength scale to declare. Making it ask for one would be demanding an answer that cannot change its result.

brainsp.atomic_data.qh_from_spectrum(wavelength, flux, ssp_flux_unit='Lsun')[source]#

Ionising photon rates of an SSP, integrated from its own spectrum.

Q = integral_0^lambda_edge L_lambda * lambda / (h c) d lambda

evaluated at the three ionisation edges, which is what sets how many photons are available to ionise H, He and He+ respectively.

This replaces the age/metallicity lookup BRAIN used previously. A lookup cannot know what is actually in the library it is being applied to: it silently returns a number for an SSP whose real ionising output is different, and there is no way for the fit to notice.

Parameters:
wavelength1D array

SSP wavelength grid in Angstrom. Must extend below 911.76 A, or there is no ionising continuum to integrate and the result is meaningless.

flux1D array

L_lambda per unit initial mass, on the same grid.

ssp_flux_unit{‘Lsun’, ‘cgs’}

Units of flux: L_sun/A/Msun or erg/s/A/Msun.

Returns:
dict

{‘QHc’, ‘QHe1’, ‘QHe2’} in photons/s per Msun formed. Entries whose edge lies outside the library’s coverage are absent, not zero — a caller must not be able to mistake “not measured” for “no photons”.

Notes

The edge bin is closed by interpolating the flux onto the edge exactly rather than truncating at the last grid point, which otherwise biases the result by the width of one bin. The integrand is interpolated logarithmically, because the Lyman continuum falls by orders of magnitude across a few Angstrom and a linear rule over the native grid overestimates it — by 2% at the He II edge on the BC03 grid.

brainsp.atomic_data.METAL_LINE_CALIBRATIONS = {'[N II]_6548': <function _nii_6548_ratio>, '[N II]_6583': <function _nii_6583_ratio>, '[Ne III]_3869': <function _neiii_3869_ratio>, '[O III]_4363': <function <lambda>>, '[O III]_4959': <function _oiii_4959_ratio>, '[O III]_5007': <function _oiii_5007_ratio>, '[O II]_3727': <function _oii_3727_ratio>, '[O I]_6300': <function _oi_6300_ratio>, '[S II]_6716': <function _sii_6716_ratio>, '[S II]_6731': <function _sii_6731_ratio>}#

Semi-empirical metal line strengths relative to H beta, keyed by catalogue name.

The whole-Angstrom wavelength that used to travel with each calibration is gone. It was a third copy of the emission-line wavelengths, rounded harder than either of the other two, and it placed the predicted lines on no scale at all. metal_calibration_wavelengths supplies the wavelength from line_catalogue on a declared scale instead.

‘[O II]_3727’ is not a catalogue entry. It is the historic name of the UNRESOLVED 3726 + 3729 blend, and _oii_3727_ratio predicts the SUM of the two, so the entry is kept under that name and the comparison against the fit sums the two fitted components. Splitting the prediction by some fixed 3729/3726 ratio would be wrong: that ratio is the density diagnostic, which is the reason the doublet is fitted as two untied lines in the first place.

brainsp.atomic_data.OII_BLEND_COMPONENTS = ('[O II]_3726', '[O II]_3729')#

The two catalogue entries whose fitted fluxes sum to the ‘[O II]_3727’ prediction.

Named here, where the prediction is defined, so that the consumer doing the summing does not have to know which components the blend is made of. The [O II] doublet used to be a single catalogue entry at 3727 and the prediction matched it by name; once the catalogue split it, the predicted name matched nothing fitted and [O II] fell silently out of the predicted-versus-observed table.

brainsp.atomic_data.metal_calibration_wavelengths(wavelength_scale)[source]#

{catalogue name: wavelength} for every metal line calibration, on a declared scale.

Every key but ‘[O II]_3727’ is a catalogue entry and takes that entry’s wavelength. ‘[O II]_3727’ names the unresolved blend and is placed at the mean of its two components, which is a label for a prediction that is itself a sum, not a claim about where either component sits.
