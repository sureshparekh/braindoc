brainsp.nebular#

BRAIN-v1 Nebular Physics Module Photoionization-based nebular continuum and emission line predictions.

Implements self-consistent nebular modeling: - Q(H) integrated from each SSP spectrum below the Lyman limit - Free-free (bremsstrahlung) emission - Free-bound (recombination continuum) emission - Two-photon continuum emission - Total nebular continuum template - Case B hydrogen emission line predictions - Semi-empirical metal emission line predictions - Self-consistency tau ratio

References: - Osterbrock & Ferland (2006) - Hummer & Storey (1987) - Nussbaumer & Schmutz (1984) - Ercolano & Storey (2006)

gamma_ff(nu, Te) [erg cm^3 s^-1 Hz^-1].

Volume emission coefficient gamma_nu, defined by

4*pi*j_nu = n_e n_p gamma_nu,

i.e. integrated over the full sphere, NOT the per-steradian j_nu.
The emission-line predictions (C_Hbeta, predict_*_lines) and the
template normalisation gamma_eff/alpha_B are on this convention, so a
per-steradian coefficient here would put the lines and the continuum
4*pi apart in scale.
The constant 6.8416e-38 is the exact theoretical coefficient
(2^5 pi e^6)/(3 m_e c^3) * sqrt(2 pi /(3 k m_e)), quoted rounded as 6.8e-38
in Rybicki & Lightman (1979) eq. 5.14b. Their eq. 5.14a, 5.44e-39, is the
same quantity divided by 4*pi.

Free-bound emission coefficient [erg cm^3 s^-1 Hz^-1].

Volume emission coefficient gamma_nu, defined by

4*pi*j_nu = n_e n_p gamma_nu,

i.e. integrated over the full sphere, NOT the per-steradian j_nu.
The emission-line predictions (C_Hbeta, predict_*_lines) and the
template normalisation gamma_eff/alpha_B are on this convention, so a
per-steradian coefficient here would put the lines and the continuum
4*pi apart in scale.
The prefactor is 8*pi*h*nu^3/c^2, from the Milne relation (Osterbrock &
Ferland 2006 eq. 2.28) combined with a Maxwellian: 2 h nu^3/c^2 is the
Planck normalisation of the per-steradian form. Checked against the Case B
photon identity, integral gamma_fb/(h nu) dnu = alpha_B, which closes to
1.3% – the residual is the n > 51 loop break below, not the prefactor.
brainsp.nebular.gamma_fb_HeI(wavelength, Te, n_max=100)[source]#

Free-bound emission coefficient for He I (approximate).

He I is not hydrogenic; we use a scaled version of H I fb emission with the He I ionization potential (24.6 eV) and effective charge. This is an approximation; detailed tables (Ercolano & Storey 2006) would be more accurate.

Volume emission coefficient gamma_nu, defined by

4*pi*j_nu = n_e n_p gamma_nu,

i.e. integrated over the full sphere, NOT the per-steradian j_nu. The emission-line predictions (C_Hbeta, predict_*_lines) and the template normalisation gamma_eff/alpha_B are on this convention, so a per-steradian coefficient here would put the lines and the continuum 4*pi apart in scale.

class brainsp.nebular.NebularModel(wavelength, wavelength_scale, Te=10000.0, ne=100.0, Y_He=0.25, Z_gas=0.02, f_esc=0.0)[source]#

Bases: object

Encapsulates all nebular physics computations.

Initialize with Te, ne, Y_He and the fitting wavelength grid. Pre-computes the normalized nebular continuum template such that:

f_neb(lambda) = template(lambda) * Q_eff

where Q_eff = sum_j w_j * unredden_j * qh_ratio_j.

brainsp.nebular.compute_qh_ratios(ages_gyr, metallicities, fbase_norms, ssp_flux_unit='Lsun', ssp_folder=None, bases_info=None, return_absolute=False, allow_partial=False)[source]#

Q(H) per unit NNLS weight for each SSP, integrated from the SSP spectra.

Each base spectrum is integrated shortwards of the hydrogen ionisation edge to get the ionising photon rate it actually emits, then divided by its own normalisation flux so the result multiplies the NNLS weight directly.

This requires the raw library files, not the templates BRAIN fits with: those have been resampled onto the observed wavelength grid, which starts thousands of Angstrom above the Lyman limit and therefore contains no ionising continuum at all.

Parameters:
ages_gyr, metallicitiesarray

Per-SSP age and metallicity. Carried for reporting only – nothing is looked up from them.

fbase_normsarray

SSP flux at the normalisation wavelength, per Msun.

ssp_flux_unit{‘Lsun’, ‘cgs’}
ssp_folderstr

Directory holding the library.

bases_infolist of dict

Base entries with a ‘filename’ key, in the same order as the arrays.

return_absolutebool

Also return the absolute rates per Msun, for reporting.

Returns:
qh_ratiosjnp array

Q(H) per unit weight. Zero for SSPs with no ionising output.

absolutedict, optional

{‘QHc’, ‘QHe1’, ‘QHe2’} arrays in photons/s per Msun.

Raises:
ValueError

If the library does not reach below the Lyman limit. Degrading quietly to a zero nebular continuum would leave a fit that looks converged and is not physically meaningful.

brainsp.nebular.extract_line_flux(gas_names, gas_weights, line_key)[source]#

Extract a specific emission line flux from the NNLS gas results.

Parameters:
gas_nameslist of str

Names of emission line templates.

gas_weightsarray

NNLS weights for gas templates.

line_keystr

A catalogue entry name, or a species from LINE_SPECIES.

Returns:
fluxfloat

Integrated line flux, summed over every entry of that species, or 0.0 if the catalogue provides none.

This matched by substring and returned the first hit until the name
couplings were broken. That returned [S II] 6716 while silently
discarding 6731, which is half the density diagnostic; returned the
[O III] 4363 auroral line in place of the 4959+5007 doublet; and
returned He II when asked for He I, because “He I” is a substring of
“He II”. It did not misfire in practice only because the two keys in
use, Halpha and Hbeta, are unambiguous in the shipped catalogue.
A species is not a line. Asking for “[O III]” returns 4363 plus the
4959+5007 doublet, because all three are [O III], and 4363 comes from a
different upper level and is not usually wanted alongside the other two.
Ask for the entry by name when a particular line is meant; an exact name
always wins over the species sum.
