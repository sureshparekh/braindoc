brainsp.line_products#

BRAIN: per-line emission products for a datacube, in the megacube layout.

The published MaNGA megacubes (Riffel et al. 2023, MNRAS 524, 5640) carry an emission-line fit. BRAIN fits the lines together with the stars, and used to keep only each kinematic group’s velocity and dispersion: the line fluxes were computed and thrown away. This writes them, with the extensions, names and definitions of those megacubes, so a BRAIN megacube reads the same way.

SOLUTION  (3N + 1, ny, nx)  A, v, sigma per line; the last plane is the
                            spaxel's chi2 / Nl_eff
DISP      (3N, ny, nx)      uncertainty of SOLUTION. NaN: not computed,
                            as in the published megacubes
INIGUESS  (3N, ny, nx)      the starting sigma; A and v are NaN, because
                            BRAIN starts from the velocity grid search
                            and an amplitude is never guessed (it is
                            solved linearly)
FLUX_M    (N, ny, nx)       line flux from the model
FLUX_D    (N, ny, nx)       line flux measured directly on the data
EQW_M     (N, ny, nx)       equivalent width from the model
EQW_D     (N, ny, nx)       equivalent width measured on the data
MASK2D    (ny, nx)          0 where a spaxel was fitted, 1 elsewhere
PARNAMES  table             (component, parameter): parameter A, v or s
SPECIDX   table             (row, column) of every fitted spaxel
FITCONFIG table             (parameters, values): rest wavelengths,
                            kinematic groups, continuum windows

THE DEFINITIONS, each checked against the published 8626-12704 megacube by rebuilding its numbers from its own SOLUTION and spectra:

FLUX_M = integral of the line model over lambda_c +- 5 sigma
         (reproduced to 2e-5)
EQW_M  = integral of -(line model) / C over the same window
         (reproduced to 3e-5)
EQW_D  = integral of 1 - data / C over the same window
         (reproduced to 1e-15)
FLUX_D = integral of data - stellar - (every other line) over it

where the window is (lambda_c - 5 s, lambda_c + 5 s) with s = sigma lambda_rest / c, and C is a straight line fitted by least squares to the stellar model in the line’s two continuum windows (blue0, blue1, red0, red1). A line with no windows falls back to a degree-7 polynomial over the whole fitting range, three passes of 3-sigma clipping. EQW is negative for an emission line.

UNITS AND FRAME

Fluxes are in the cube’s own flux unit times Angstrom, and OBSERVED: the line as it is in the data, attenuated by the fitted dust like everything else in the model. That is the published megacubes’ FLUX_M, and it is not BRAIN’s fitted weight, which is the line before attenuation. The wavelength frame is the frame the fit ran in: the rest frame when BRAIN removed a redshift, with the flux density not rescaled by (1 + z).

A is the peak of the Gaussian whose integral is the line’s total observed flux, so FLUX_M = A sqrt(2 pi) s lambda_c / c up to the 5.7e-7 of a Gaussian that lies beyond 5 sigma; v and s are the line’s kinematic group’s, observed (no instrumental correction).

THE LINE MODEL is BRAIN’s own: the pixel-integrated Gaussian of emission_lines._build_emission_jit, rebuilt here per component in numpy, so the products are integrals of exactly the profile that was fitted.

brainsp.line_products.components(table, doublets)[source]#

Every line BRAIN fits, one entry per emitting line, in wavelength order.

A tied doublet is one fitted column and one weight, the stronger line’s flux; it is two lines here, each with its own flux, because every reader of a line map want [O III] 4959 and 5007 separately.

brainsp.line_products.unit_profile(lam, velscale, rest, sigma, v0)[source]#

One line’s profile on the log-lambda grid, unit area in Angstrom.

The same construction as emission_lines._build_emission_jit: the analytic transform of a Gaussian times that of a one-pixel top hat, transformed back over N_pix and divided by the pixel width in Angstrom.

brainsp.line_products.continuum(lam, stellar, name, fallback=None)[source]#

C(lambda): the continuum an equivalent width is measured against.

fallback is the no-window continuum, which depends only on the stellar model, so a caller measuring many lines fits it once and passes it in.

brainsp.line_products.measure(lam, velscale, data, stellar, params, gas_weights, n_groups, dk, comps)[source]#

Every product for one spaxel, in the spaxel’s normalised units.

Returns arrays over comps: A, v, s, flux_m, flux_d, eqw_m, eqw_d. Fluxes are per normalised unit times Angstrom; the caller multiplies by the spaxel’s normalisation to reach the cube’s units. EQWs are unitless ratios integrated over Angstrom and need no scaling.

brainsp.line_products.hdus(lam, velscale, data, stellar, params, weights, n_ssp, norm, chi2, n_groups, dk, table, doublets, vi, ny, nx, wavelength_scale=None, sigma_start=nan, progress=None)[source]#

The line products of a fitted cube as a list of HDUs.

data, stellar: (n_spaxel, n_pix) on the log grid, normalised. params: (n_spaxel, 4 + 2 n_groups). weights: (n_spaxel, n_ssp + n_gas). norm: (n_spaxel,) the factor that turns normalised flux into cube flux. vi: flat spaxel index of each fitted spaxel. Returns [] with no lines.
