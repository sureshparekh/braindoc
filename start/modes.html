The three fitting modes#

One setting, nebular_mode, decides how much of the light BRAIN attributes to ionised gas. Choose it first: the template library you can use, the settings you must give and the products you get all depend on it.

What each mode fits. The dashed curve in nespop is the nebular continuum, a smooth light that the ionising photons of the young stars produce and that the other two modes cannot represent.

Set it the same way everywhere:

Model step, panel Nebular treatment, at the top of the model. Choosing nespop selects a library that can run it and greys out those that cannot.

r = brainsp.fit_spectrum("galaxy.spec", nebular_mode="espop",
                         wavelength_scale="vacuum")

brainsp fit galaxy.spec --set nebular_mode=espop --set wavelength_scale=vacuum

nebular_mode: espop
wavelength_scale: vacuum

spop: the stars alone#

The emission lines are not fitted. The model is stars, dust and the stellar line-of-sight velocity distribution, and nothing else, which makes spop the fastest mode and the right one for a passive galaxy. Where a spectrum does have lines, mask them: an unmasked line is light the stars must try to reproduce, and it drags the continuum. The Standard optical mask of the interface, or brainsp.STANDARD_OPTICAL_MASK in Python, masks the usual optical lines in one step (Wavelength masks).

espop: the stars and the emission lines#

Every line of the catalogue that falls in the fitted range gets a free, non-negative amplitude, and each group of lines gets its own velocity and dispersion, independent of the stars. No photoionisation model is involved, so espop works with every template library.

The fit follows the sequence FADO uses, in four stages:

  1. The stellar kinematics from the stars alone. \(v_\star\) and \(\sigma_\star\) come from a fit with the strong lines masked over ±1500 km s⁻¹ (kinematics_mask_width), so that no gas line can pull them.

  2. The stars and the strong lines together. This fit gives the gas kinematics and decides which lines are detected.

  3. The populations on the line-free pixels. The stars are fitted again away from every detected line, so that the populations cannot absorb a line’s wings.

  4. Every line measured on the data minus the stellar continuum, the faint ones included, at the gas kinematics of its group.

You must say whether the spectrum’s wavelengths are in air or in vacuum (wavelength_scale). The two differ by about 83 km s⁻¹ across the optical, nearly constant in velocity, so a wrong guess would appear only as a spurious offset between the gas and the stars; BRAIN therefore refuses to guess. SDSS, MaNGA, DESI and LAMOST deliver vacuum wavelengths; MUSE and CALIFA deliver air.

Warning

espop has no nebular continuum. Where the gas is bright, its continuum has nowhere to go in the model and is absorbed by the young populations and the extinction. If the lines are strong and your library reaches the Lyman limit, use nespop.

nespop: the nebular continuum as well#

As in FADO (Gomes & Papaderos 2017), every stellar population carries the nebular continuum of the gas its own Lyman-continuum photons keep ionised: Case B recombination, every photon absorbed, at the measured electron temperature and density, reddened by the gas extinction and broadened with the Balmer lines. The continuum has no free amplitude; its strength follows from the populations the fit chooses. Where the gas is ionised by stars and the lines are reliable, H\(\alpha\) and H\(\beta\) are tied to the same photons (FADO’s FC treatment); elsewhere the nebular continuum is fitted without that tie (NC).

nespop reports, beyond espop, the nebular fraction of the light \(y_{\rm neb}\), the ionising photon rate \(Q(\mathrm{H})\), the gas extinction \(A_V^{\rm neb}\) and how it was obtained, \(T_e\) and \(n_e\), and the ratio of predicted to measured H\(\beta\).

Important

nespop integrates each template’s spectrum below 911.76 Å to count its ionising photons, so it needs a library that reaches that far. BC03 is the library nespop has been validated with. M13 reaches the Lyman limit too, and BRAIN runs it with a warning that its ionising photons are untested. BasesGM stops in the optical, and BRAIN refuses nespop with it rather than fit a nebular component that would be identically zero. brainsp libraries info NAME reports whether a library reaches the Lyman limit; Template libraries explains how to add one.

The nebular emission describes each stage of the nespop fit, its tests and its accuracy on mocks and on real data.

Which mode to choose#

Your spectrum

Mode

no emission lines, or only weak ones

spop, with the lines masked

emission lines, any template library

emission lines with broad wings (outflows)

strong lines, a library reaching the Lyman limit

strong lines, fitted range starting redward of the Balmer jump (3646 Å)

nespop, knowing that the nebular continuum and the dust are then partly degenerate

Note

The modes once called nepop and photopop no longer exist. nespop is the former photopop, and a spectrum that the FC treatment cannot be applied to is fitted within nespop with the nebular continuum alone, which is what nepop did. A settings file naming either old mode is refused with this advice.
