Key concepts#

The words the rest of these pages use, each in a few lines, in the order you meet them.

The data#

Spectrum

A one-dimensional galaxy spectrum: wavelength in Å, flux, and ideally an error and a quality flag for every pixel, in a plain text file. Your data gives the format.

Datacube

An integral-field (IFU) observation, a spectrum at every spaxel of a two-dimensional field, in a FITS file (MaNGA, MUSE, CALIFA, JWST NIRSpec, …).

Spaxel

One spatial element of a datacube, with its own spectrum. BRAIN fits every spaxel independently.

Native pixels

BRAIN compares the model with the data on the data’s own pixels, never on a resampled copy. Resampling blurs the lines and correlates the noise, which biases the dispersions and makes \(\chi^2\) look better than it is; Native pixels shows by how much.

The model#

Template library (or base)

A set of simple stellar population (SSP) spectra: the light of stars of one age and one metallicity, all born at once. BRAIN ships BasesGM (84 SSPs, 3540 to 6996 Å), M13 (47 SSPs, from the far ultraviolet into the infrared) and BC03 (100 SSPs, from the far ultraviolet into the infrared), and you can add others (Template libraries).

Full spectral fitting

Finding the non-negative mixture of templates, reddened by dust and shifted and broadened by the stars’ motions, that reproduces the spectrum pixel by pixel. The model writes down the model.

Light fraction \(x_j\)

The share of the light at the normalisation wavelength (5500 Å by default) that template \(j\) contributes. A light fraction is not a mass fraction: a young population is far brighter per unit mass than an old one.

Mass fraction \(\mu_j\)

The share of the stellar mass in template \(j\). The initial mass (all ever formed) and the present mass (still in stars and remnants) differ by what the stars returned to the gas.

Extinction \(A_V\)

Magnitudes of absorption by dust in the V band, with a wavelength dependence set by an extinction law (Cardelli, Clayton & Mathis 1989 by default). \(Y_V\) is an extra extinction for the youngest populations only.

Kinematics

The line-of-sight velocity \(v_\star\) and velocity dispersion \(\sigma_\star\) of the stars, and the same for each kinematic group of emission lines.

Fitting mode (nebular_mode)

How much of the light is attributed to ionised gas: spop (stars only), espop (and emission lines), nespop (and the nebular continuum). See The three fitting modes.

The steps around the fit#

Corrections

What BRAIN undoes before fitting: the redshift, and the reddening by the Milky Way’s dust (the Galactic \(E(B-V)\)). The galaxy’s own dust is not a correction; it is fitted.

Preparation (datacubes)

Optional changes to a datacube before the fit: binning spaxels, binning or degrading the spectral axis, a spatial filter. Pre-fitting stages describes each.

Mask

Wavelength regions left out of the fit (weight 0) or counted more (weight above 1). For a datacube, a signal-to-noise mask also chooses which spaxels are fitted.

Settings

Every choice BRAIN makes, 83 of them, each with a default: a Settings object in Python, a YAML file, or the controls of the interface. Settings lists them all.

What comes out#

Summary file

The text file a spectrum’s fit writes: every fitted and derived quantity, the population vector and the uncertainties (What a fit writes).

Megacube

The FITS file a datacube’s fit writes: the input cube with, for every spaxel, the observed and model spectra, the population vectors, a map of every fitted and derived quantity, the emission-line products and their errors (Megacube files).

Formal and bootstrap errors

The two ways BRAIN computes uncertainties: from the fit’s own covariance (fast, default) or by refitting many noise-resampled copies of the data (slower, error_samples). Uncertainties says which to trust for what.

Device

Where JAX runs the fit: the CPU, a GPU, or auto (a GPU when one is visible). The choice changes the speed, never the numbers (CPUs and GPUs).
