# The linear solve

For fixed non-linear parameters $\theta$, the model is linear in the weights:
$M = A(\theta)\, w$, where column $j$ of $A$ is template $j$ broadened, shifted
and attenuated (and the line and nebular columns). The weights solve

$$ \min_{w \ge 0} \; \lVert W^{1/2}(A w - f) \rVert^2 + \lVert D w_\star \rVert^2, $$

$W = \mathrm{diag}(m_\lambda/\sigma_\lambda^2)$, which is a non-negative
least-squares problem on the augmented system

$$ \tilde A = \begin{pmatrix} W^{1/2} A \\ D\; 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \tilde f = \begin{pmatrix} W^{1/2} f \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

## Active-set NNLS

`linear_solver: active_set` (the default) solves it exactly with the
Lawson–Hanson active-set method on the normal equations $\tilde A^{\rm
T}\tilde A\, w = \tilde A^{\rm T}\tilde f$: weights move between an active set
(held at zero) and a passive set (solved by unconstrained least squares) until
the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions hold. It reaches scipy's NNLS optimum to
$\sim 10^{-12}$. Implemented in JAX with fixed-size loops so it compiles and
vectorises over spaxels. A datacube's fast fitter starts each solve from the
previous one's active set, which gives the same answer in fewer iterations.

The solution is sparse: typically 5–20 of 84 templates carry light. That is
the honest answer of a degenerate problem, not a failure to converge.

`fista` is an older iterative solver kept to reproduce old results; it does
not converge on this problem.

## Double precision

$\tilde A^{\rm T}\tilde A$ has condition numbers near $10^{10}$ (templates of
neighbouring age and metallicity are nearly collinear), so the solve needs
float64; in float32 the weights change. BRAIN enables JAX's 64-bit mode
everywhere.

## Regularisation

`smoothness` $\lambda_s$ (default 0, off) adds a Tikhonov penalty on the
stellar weights, $\lVert D w_\star\rVert^2$, with $D$ the first- or
second-difference operator (`smoothness_order`):

$$ (D_1 w)_j = \lambda_s (w_{j+1} - w_j), \qquad (D_2 w)_j = \lambda_s (w_{j+2} - 2w_{j+1} + w_j), $$

over the templates in catalogue order (sorted by metallicity, then age), so
it favours star-formation histories that vary smoothly with age. It biases the
answer towards smoothness by design; use it to stabilise histories at low
signal-to-noise, and report it.

## The simplex constraint

`enforce_simplex: true` adds $\sum_{j\le N_\star} w_j = 1$ to the system (via the
KKT equations). Off by default: the constraint is only right together with a
fitted normalisation, and the reported fractions are normalised to 100 per
cent either way.
