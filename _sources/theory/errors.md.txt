# Uncertainties

## Light fractions

With `error_samples` $N > 0$ BRAIN estimates the uncertainty of every weight
by Monte Carlo: the spectrum is perturbed $N$ times by Gaussian noise of the
quoted errors (or, without errors, by resampling the residuals), the linear
problem is solved for each with the non-linear parameters held at the best
fit, by the same solver as the fit, and the standard deviation of each weight
over the resamples is reported (the `Err` column of the line table, and
`w_err` internally). The resamples are solved all at once, vectorised.

:::{warning}
These are not calibrated confidence intervals. They measure how far the
weights move under the errors *as given*: with $\chi^2/N_{\rm eff}$ well below
1 the quoted errors are too large and so are these bars, and they do not
include the uncertainty of the non-linear parameters, which are held fixed.
:::

## Non-linear parameters

No formal uncertainties are reported for $A_V$, $v_\star$, $\sigma_\star$ or
the gas kinematics. Practical estimates:

- fit mock spectra like yours with {mod}`brainsp.mock` at your
  signal-to-noise and look at the scatter (the self-test does this; see
  [tutorial 7](../tutorials/datacube.md) for a cube);
- for a datacube, the spaxel-to-spaxel scatter of a smooth map.

## Degeneracies

Age and metallicity, and age and dust, trade against each other: an older,
metal-poorer population looks like a younger, metal-richer one, and a little
more dust like a little older. Individual $x_j$ are therefore poorly
determined, while averages over them ($\langle\log t\rangle$, light in broad
age bins) are robust. Report those.
