# Emission lines

In `espop`, `nepop` and `photopop` every line of the catalogue that can reach
the fitted range is a column of the linear system, with a free non-negative
amplitude, and its velocity and dispersion are non-linear parameters shared
with the other lines of its **kinematic group**.

## The line profile

Each line is a Gaussian in $\ln\lambda$ of dispersion $\sigma_g$ (km/s)
centred on $\lambda_{\rm rest}(1 + v_g/c)$, **integrated over each pixel**,
not sampled at pixel centres. It is built in Fourier space as the product of
a Gaussian's transform and the transform of a one-pixel top hat,

$$ \tilde g(\omega) = \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(\omega x_\sigma)^2 - i\,\omega x_0\right]\, \frac{\sin(\omega/2)}{\omega/2}, $$

with $x_0$ the line centre and $x_\sigma = \sigma_g/\Delta v$ its width, both in
pixels (Westfall et al. 2019, §9.1.2). The profile has unit area at any
width, including below a pixel; sampling a Gaussian at pixel centres would
lose most of a narrow line's flux and force a floor on $\sigma_g$. The column
is attenuated by the stars' $r_\lambda(A_V)$ ([](dust.md)), so its fitted
weight is the line's flux before that dust.

## The catalogue

Lines are stored once, at **vacuum** wavelengths from NIST (H$_2$ from Roueff et
al. 2019); air wavelengths are derived from them with Ciddor (1996). Which set
is used is `wavelength_scale`, a fact about the *observed* spectrum: air and
vacuum differ by about 83 km/s across the optical, and a wrong choice moves
every gas velocity by that much with no other symptom, so it is required
rather than guessed. The optical lines fitted:

[O II] 3726, 3729; H10, H9, H8; [Ne III] 3869; H$\epsilon$, H$\delta$,
H$\gamma$; [O III] 4363; He II 4686; H$\beta$; [O III] 4959, 5007; He I 5876;
[O I] 6300; [N II] 6548, 6583; H$\alpha$; [S II] 6716, 6731. The near-infrared
catalogue (Paschen, Brackett, [S III], He I 10830, H$_2$, [Fe II] ...) is used
when the range reaches it.

A line that cannot land on the fitted grid at any velocity and dispersion
inside the bounds is left out (and named in the log), rather than fitted as a
column of zeros: so a flux of zero in the results is a real non-detection.

## Tied doublets

[O III] 4959/5007 and [N II] 6548/6583 (and [S III] 9069/9531) come from one
upper level each, so their ratio is fixed by atomic physics independently of
temperature and density. Each pair is one column, the ratio-weighted sum of
the two profiles:

| Doublet | Ratio (weak/strong) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [O III] 4959/5007 | 0.33510 | Storey & Zeippen 2000 |
| [N II] 6548/6583 | 0.32739 | Storey & Zeippen 2000 |
| [S III] 9069/9531 | 0.40506 | Froese Fischer, Tachiev & Irimia 2006 |

The fitted weight is the strong line's flux, as the BPT calibrations use.
[O II] 3726/3729 and [S II] 6716/6731 are density diagnostics and are left
free.

## Kinematic groups

Lines in one group share $(v_g, \sigma_g)$. By default the recombination and
low-ionisation lines (H$\alpha$, H$\beta$, [N II], [S II], ...) form one group
and the high-ionisation lines ([O III], [Ne III], ...) another; `line_groups`
sets them explicitly:

```yaml
line_groups:
  0: [Halpha, Hbeta, "[N II]", "[S II]"]
  1: ["[O III]", "[Ne III]"]
```

A group none of whose lines carries flux is reported as `unconstrained`
rather than with numbers the optimiser happened to stop at.
