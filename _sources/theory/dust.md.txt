# Dust

## Attenuation

Every stellar template, and the emission lines, are attenuated by

$$ r_\lambda(A_V) = 10^{-0.4\, A_V \,[\,q(\lambda) - q(\lambda_0)\,]}, \qquad q(\lambda) = A_\lambda / A_V , $$

a screen of dust normalised at $\lambda_0$, so that $A_V$ changes the
continuum's *shape* and the light fractions at $\lambda_0$ stay light
fractions. $A_V$ is fitted within `dust_range` ([-1, 5] mag by default). A
negative $A_V$ is allowed on purpose: light reaching us through the galaxy
itself (from its far side, say) has crossed its dust, and correcting for
that needs a model of the geometry that is made differently by every group,
so it is common practice to let $A_V$ go down to $-1$ instead. Set
`dust_range: [0, 5]` to forbid it. The templates
flagged young in the library's catalogue receive $A_V + Y_V$, with $Y_V$
fitted within `young_extra_dust_range`; BasesGM flags none, so $Y_V$ is
exactly 0 there.

The nebular continuum is not attenuated by $A_V$ (it carries its own
extinction in `photopop`); the emission lines are, which is why a spectrum's
line fluxes are reported corrected for it ([](../user_guide/outputs.md)).

## The laws

`reddening_law` (default CCM). All twelve:

| Key | Reference | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `CCM` | Cardelli, Clayton & Mathis (1989) | Milky Way average, $R_V = 3.1$: the default |
| `HZ1` | Allen (1976) | Milky Way, tabulated |
| `HZ2` | Seaton (1979), fit by Fitzpatrick (1986) | Milky Way |
| `CAL` | Calzetti et al. (2000) | starbursts, $R_V = 4.05$ |
| `CCC` | Calzetti (2000) + Leitherer (2002) | starbursts, far-UV extension below 1846 Å |
| `HZ5` | Calzetti (2000), starburst (SB) form | the same curve as `CAL` |
| `HZ3` | Fitzpatrick (1986) | Large Magellanic Cloud |
| `GD3` | Gordon et al. (2003) | LMC average |
| `GD2` | Gordon et al. (2003) | LMC2 supershell, near 30 Doradus |
| `HZ4` | Prévot et al. (1984), Bouchet et al. (1985) | Small Magellanic Cloud |
| `GD1` | Gordon et al. (2003) | SMC bar: steep far-UV, no 2175 Å bump |
| `GD24` | Gordon et al. (2024) | SMC |

The CCM law, for $x = 1/\lambda$ in $\mu$m$^{-1}$:

$$ q(\lambda) = a(x) + b(x)/R_V, $$

with, in the optical and near-infrared ($1.1 \le x < 3.3$, $y = x - 1.82$),

$$ a = 1 + 0.17699y - 0.50447y^2 - 0.02427y^3 + 0.72085y^4 + 0.01979y^5 - 0.77530y^6 + 0.32999y^7, $$
$$ b = 1.41338y + 2.28305y^2 + 1.07233y^3 - 5.38434y^4 - 0.62251y^5 + 5.30260y^6 - 2.09002y^7, $$

$a = 0.574x^{1.61}$, $b = -0.527x^{1.61}$ in the infrared ($0.3 \le x < 1.1$), and
the ultraviolet branches of Cardelli et al. above $x = 3.3$.

The curves differ mostly in the ultraviolet; fitted over 3700–6900 Å they
give similar answers. On one MaNGA spectrum: $A_V$ = 0.220 (CCM), 0.254
(CAL), 0.188 (GD1), 0.211 (GD24), with $\chi^2/N$ within 0.3 per cent.

## Galactic dust

The Milky Way's reddening is not fitted: it is removed before the fit, at the
observed wavelengths, with $A_\lambda = R_V E(B-V) q(\lambda)$
([](../user_guide/corrections.md)).
