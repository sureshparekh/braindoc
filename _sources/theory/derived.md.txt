# Derived quantities

From the weights $w_j$ of the $N_\star$ stellar templates (ages $t_j$,
metallicities $Z_j$).

## Light fractions

$$ x_j = 100\,\frac{w_j}{\sum_{i} w_i} \quad[\%], $$

summed over every component, AGN included: the share of the attenuated model
at $\lambda_0$.

## Masses

A weight is a flux at $\lambda_0$; a template's library flux per unit initial
mass there is $\phi_j$ (`fbase_norm`, $(L/M)_j$ in the summary). Undoing the
attenuation that template suffered,

$$ M^{\rm ini}_j = \frac{w_j\, F_{\lambda_0}\, 10^{\,0.4\,(A_V + Y_j)\, q(\lambda_0)}}{\phi_j}, \qquad M^{\rm cor}_j = M^{\rm ini}_j\, m^\star_j , $$

$m^\star_j$ being the fraction of the initial mass still in stars and remnants
at $t_j$ (`MSTARS`). The factor $10^{0.4 (A_V+Y_j) q(\lambda_0)}$ undoes the
extinction the template suffered at $\lambda_0$ itself: the attenuation in the
model is normalised there, so the weight is the template's *attenuated* flux
at $\lambda_0$. Leaving it out would make the dustiest populations look the
lightest. The mass fractions are $\mu_j = 100\, M_j / \sum_i M_i$ over the
**stellar** templates only: an AGN component has light and no mass. The
totals `Mini_tot`, `Mcor_tot` are **apparent**: multiply by the flux unit
times $4\pi d^2 / L_\odot$ for solar masses (BRAIN uses no distance).

## Mean ages and metallicities

$$ \langle \log t \rangle_L = \frac{\sum_j x_j \log_{10} t_j}{\sum_j x_j}, \qquad \langle \log t \rangle_M = \frac{\sum_j \mu_j \log_{10} t_j}{\sum_j \mu_j}, $$

$$ \langle Z \rangle_L = \frac{\sum_j x_j Z_j}{\sum_j x_j}, \qquad \langle Z \rangle_M = \frac{\sum_j \mu_j Z_j}{\sum_j \mu_j}, $$

over the stellar templates (present mass in the mass-weighted ones). These
are the means of $\log t$, not the logarithm of the mean age, and of $Z$ as a
mass fraction, not of [Z/H]. From a fit: `FitResult.mean_log_age_light` and
so on. In a megacube (`Mage_L`, `Mage_M`, `MZ_L`, `MZ_M`) the light-weighted
pair keeps the definition of the published megacubes (Riffel et al. 2023):
the numerator over the stellar templates, the denominator over every
component, so featureless (AGN) light lowers them.

## Population bins

A megacube's `PoPBins` carries the light and mass in broad age bins, each
`[name, youngest, oldest]` in years, a template belonging to a bin when
$\text{youngest} < t_j \le \text{oldest}$. The default is the six MaNGA bins:

| Bin | Ages (yr) |
|---|---|
| `xyy` | $9\times10^5 < t \le 10^7$ |
| `xyo` | $1.4\times10^7 < t \le 5.6\times10^7$ |
| `xiy` | $10^8 < t \le 5\times10^8$ |
| `xii` | $6.3\times10^8 < t \le 8\times10^8$ |
| `xio` | $8.9\times10^8 < t \le 2\times10^9$ |
| `xo` | $5\times10^9 < t \le 1.3\times10^{10}$ |

as `<bin>_light` and `<bin>_mass` planes (per cent).

## Equivalent widths and line fluxes of a datacube

Over $\lambda_c \pm 5\sigma$ of each line:

$$ F_M = \int \ell(\lambda)\, d\lambda, \qquad EW_M = \int -\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{C(\lambda)}\, d\lambda, \qquad EW_D = \int \Big(1 - \frac{f(\lambda)}{C(\lambda)}\Big) d\lambda, $$

$\ell$ the line's model profile (observed, attenuated), $f$ the data and $C$ a
straight line fitted to the stellar model in the line's continuum windows.
Equivalent widths are negative for emission.
