# 3. An AGN continuum

An active nucleus adds a featureless continuum, roughly a power law
$F_\nu \propto \nu^{-\alpha}$, which no stellar population can reproduce. A
fit without it fakes the blue part of that continuum with young stars and the
red part with dust. `mock_seyfert_agn.spec` is an old host with a power law
($\alpha = 1.5$) carrying 25 per cent of the light at 5500 Å, and Seyfert
emission lines.

`include_power_law=True` adds seven featureless templates to whichever
library is used: power laws with $\alpha$ = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0
and Cloudy's AGN incident continuum for big-bump
temperatures of $1$, $1.5$ and $2 \times 10^5$ K. See
[](../user_guide/agn.md).

Without the AGN templates the fit invents 7.4 per cent of the light in
populations younger than 100 Myr and makes the mean age 0.4 dex too young
and $A_V$ 0.22 mag too high. With them, the AGN takes 24.3 per cent (truth
25), the young population disappears, and age and extinction come back to
the truth.

Only the *sum* of the AGN components is a measurement: in the optical the
Cloudy continua are power laws of slope 0.59 to 0.74 to within 1.4 per
cent, so how the light is split between neighbouring AGN templates is set by
noise. `r.agn_fraction` is that sum.
