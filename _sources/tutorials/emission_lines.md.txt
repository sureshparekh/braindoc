# 2. Emission lines

`mock_star_forming_lines.spec` is a young-to-intermediate population with an
H II-region emission-line spectrum on top, the gas moving at 50 km/s with a
dispersion of 60 km/s. Fitting it needs a *nebular mode* that models the
lines, `espop`, and the wavelength scale the spectrum is on, because the
lines are placed at rest wavelengths that differ between air and vacuum by
about 83 km/s (see [](../user_guide/modes.md)).

## What the line fluxes are

Two conventions are in play, and the summary file states both:

1. They are **relative to the normalised spectrum**: the continuum is 1 at
   the normalisation wavelength (5500 Å), so a flux of 6 is six times the
   continuum flux density at 5500 Å times one Ångström. Multiply by
   `fobs_norm` (`r.fobs_norm`) for the units of the input spectrum:
   `r.line_fluxes(units="input")`.
2. They are **corrected for the stellar extinction**, relative to the
   normalisation wavelength: each is multiplied by
   $10^{0.4 A_V [q(\lambda) - q(\lambda_0)]}$. That is why the
   "written" column is above the truth in the blue ([O II], H$\delta$) and
   below it in the red (H$\alpha$). `r.line_fluxes(observed=True)` undoes
   it and gives the flux as it arrives.

As observed, the strong lines come back within a few per cent of the
truth: H$\alpha$ 1.03, H$\beta$ 1.03, [N II] 1.07. The weak ones scatter
more: He I 5876 at 0.87 and [S II] 6731 at 0.95, because they are a few per
cent of the continuum. The tied doublets
[O III] 4959,5007 and [N II] 6548,6583 are fitted as one profile each at the
ratio atomic physics fixes, and reported under the doublet's name and under
each member's.

The gas dispersion comes back at 63 km/s against a true 60: the mock is
sampled on a 1 Å grid, which adds about 13 km/s in quadrature at H$\alpha$.
