# Pre-fit corrections

Two things happen to a galaxy's light on its way to the telescope that BRAIN
undoes *before* it fits, in the reverse of the order they happened:

1. **The Milky Way's dust** reddened the light as it arrived, so it is removed
   at the **observed** wavelengths, first.
2. **The redshift** stretched the wavelengths; they are divided by $1 + z$.

The galaxy's own dust is not a correction: it is a fitted parameter, $A_V$,
and removing any of it beforehand would be undone or double-counted by the
fit.

## Galactic reddening

The flux and its error are both multiplied by

$$ f(\lambda_{\rm obs}) = 10^{\,0.4\, R_V\, E(B-V)\, q(\lambda_{\rm obs})}, $$

where $q = A_\lambda / A_V$ is the chosen law (CCM by default, $R_V = 3.1$),
so the signal-to-noise of every pixel is unchanged. $E(B-V)$ for a
position on the sky comes from the dust maps (Schlegel, Finkbeiner & Davis
1998, recalibrated by Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011; the IRSA Galactic Dust
Reddening service gives both).

| | Python / settings | Interface |
|---|---|---|
| $E(B-V)$ | `galactic_ebv` (0 = off) | Corrections > Galactic E(B−V) |
| $R_V$ | `galactic_rv` (3.1) | R_V |
| the law | `galactic_law` (CCM) | Law |

A spectrum already in the rest frame still has its Galactic reddening
removed at the observed wavelength $\lambda_{\rm rest}(1+z)$, which is why
`spectrum_redshift` matters to this correction either way.

## The redshift

| `spectrum_in_rest_frame` | `spectrum_redshift` means |
|---|---|
| `True` (default) | the redshift that was already removed; recorded, not applied |
| `False` | the redshift BRAIN removes: wavelengths ÷ $(1+z)$ |

The flux density is not rescaled by $1+z$: BRAIN fits a normalised spectrum,
and a cube is not rescaled either, so a spectrum and a cube of the same
galaxy are treated alike. The redshift that was removed also sets the
observed frame the instrumental resolution is applied in
([](resolution.md)).

For a **datacube**, `redshift=` (Python), `--redshift` (command line) or
*Corrections > Redshift* (interface) is the cube's redshift, always removed;
the cube is taken to be in the observed frame. Galactic dereddening is the
pipeline's first stage (`galactic_ebv=`).

## Per-spectrum and per-cube values

A table carries each one's own values; see [](batches.md) for spectra
(`filename, redshift, ebv, rv, law, rest_frame`) and [](datacubes.md) for
cubes (`target, redshift, ebv, rv, law, ...`).

## In the output

The summary file records both: `E(B-V) Galactic, removed before fitting`
with the law and $R_V$, and who removed the redshift (`the user` or `BRAIN`).
