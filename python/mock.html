PythonTools

Mock data#

brainsp.mock builds spectra and datacubes from the template library with known properties, so that you can see whether a fit recovers them. The examples, the tutorials and the self-test all use it.

The examples#

import brainsp
folder = brainsp.mock.write_examples()          # ~/.brainsp/examples

examples/
├── spectra/
│   ├── mock_old_population.spec          an old, metal-rich population
│   ├── mock_young_mixture.spec           a young and an old population mixed
│   ├── mock_star_forming_lines.spec      a star-forming galaxy with H II-region lines
│   ├── mock_seyfert_agn.spec             a Seyfert: stars, an AGN continuum and Seyfert lines
│   ├── mock_observed_frame.spec          the old population at z = 0.03 behind Galactic dust
│   ├── *.truth.json                      what each was built with
│   └── corrections.csv                   the per-spectrum corrections table
├── masks/
│   └── emission_lines.sm                 the standard optical windows
└── cube/
    ├── mock_cube.fits                    16 × 16 spaxels, MaNGA-like, z = 0.02
    └── mock_cube.truth.fits              the maps it was built from

write_examples(folder=None, cube=True, overwrite=False) writes them once; later calls reuse what is there unless overwrite=True. On the command line: brainsp examples [FOLDER].

Each spectrum’s .truth.json holds everything it was built with: the components and their light fractions, \(A_V\), \(v_\star\), \(\sigma_\star\), the S/N, the light-weighted mean age and metallicity, the gas lines and kinematics, the redshift, the Galactic reddening and the wavelength scale.

A spectrum of your own#

wave, flux, error, flag, truth = brainsp.mock.make_spectrum(
    population=[(1e7, 0.019, 0.3), (1e10, 0.019, 0.7)],
    av=0.5, v=100.0, sigma=120.0, sn=40.0,
    lines="star forming", halpha=30.0, v_gas=110.0, sigma_gas=60.0, seed=3)
brainsp.mock.write_spectrum("my_mock.spec", wave, flux, error, flag, truth)
r = brainsp.fit_spectrum("my_mock.spec", nebular_mode="espop", wavelength_scale="vacuum")

Parameter

Meaning

population

[(age_yr, Z, light_fraction), ...]; the nearest template of the library is used, and the fractions are rescaled to sum to 1 - agn

av

the galaxy’s extinction, mag

v, sigma

stellar velocity and dispersion, km s⁻¹

sn

S/N per pixel at 5500 Å (rest)

agn

light fraction at 5500 Å of an \(F_\nu\propto\nu^{-1.5}\) power law

lines

"star forming", "seyfert", "liner" or None: the emission-line ratios

halpha

the H\(\alpha\) flux, in units of the continuum at 5500 Å times Å (roughly its equivalent width)

v_gas, sigma_gas

gas velocity and dispersion, km s⁻¹

redshift, galactic_ebv

the wavelengths are multiplied by \(1+z\), and Milky Way reddening is applied at the observed wavelengths

law, library

the galaxy’s extinction law, and the template library

step, flag_fraction, seed

the pixel size in Å, the fraction of pixels flagged bad at random, and the noise seed

It returns the wavelength, flux, error and flag arrays and the truth dictionary.

A datacube of your own#

brainsp.mock.write_cube("my_cube.fits", n=24, redshift=0.03, seed=2)
wave, flux, ivar, mask, truth = brainsp.mock.make_cube(n=16, redshift=0.02)

The mock cube is MaNGA-like: wavelengths sampled logarithmically (\(d\log_{10}\lambda = 10^{-4}\)), FLUX, IVAR and MASK extensions, bit 10 of the mask set outside the field and on randomly flagged pixels, and maps of velocity, dispersion, extinction and populations that vary across the field (cube_truth(n, redshift) returns them). write_cube also writes <name>.truth.fits with those maps.
