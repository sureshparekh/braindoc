Pre-fitting stages#

Before a datacube is fitted, it can be binned, degraded, filtered and dereddened, and masked by signal-to-noise. Each stage is a keyword of CubePipeline, off unless given; each writes its result into the megacube as a new extension, beside the original data, and the next stage reads it.

The stages in the order they run, whatever order the keywords are given in.

Spatial binning#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02, spatial_binning=4)

Square blocks of n × n spaxels are summed into one, on the flux and on the uncertainty together, combined correctly for the uncertainty’s kind. The quality mask is dropped, since a flag cannot be summed.

Resolution#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02,
                     degrade_resolution={"input type": "R", "input value": 2200,
                                         "output type": "FWHM", "output value": 6.0})

The data are convolved down to a coarser resolution. The types are "R" (resolving power), "FWHM" and "sigma" (in Å); "input ref wave" and "output ref wave" give the wavelength a resolving power refers to when it varies.

Warning

This smooths the data and correlates neighbouring pixels, so \(\chi^2\) and every uncertainty after it are optimistic. BRAIN’s own route is instrumental_resolution, which convolves the templates instead (Emission lines and resolution); asking for both is refused. Use this stage only to reproduce someone else’s processing.

Spectral binning#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02, spectral_binning=2.0)

The cube is put on a uniform step of this many Å, flux and uncertainty together, by linear interpolation over the finite pixels. BRAIN does not need it, since it fits the data on their own pixels (Native pixels), and resampling correlates the noise; use it only to match another analysis.

Butterworth filter#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02, butterworth={"order": 3, "range": 0.3})

A low-pass filter over the two spatial axes, nothing along the spectrum, with the transfer function \(H = 1 - 1/[1 + (\mathrm{range}/r)^{2\,\mathrm{order}}]\) on the normalised spatial-frequency radius \(r\). At the defaults one spaxel’s light spreads over about six neighbours; the fraction of the flux that survives is written to the header keyword BW_KEPT and logged. The noise after it is correlated across the field.

Galactic dereddening#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02,
                     galactic_ebv=0.05, galactic_rv=3.1, galactic_law="CCM")

The Milky Way’s extinction, \(A_V = R_V\,E(B-V)\), removed from the flux with any of BRAIN’s extinction laws.

S/N masks#

brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02,
                     sn_mask={"thresholds": [3, 10], "sn window": [5650, 5750],
                              "method": "ivar"},
                     fit_sn_threshold=3)

One integer plane per threshold, SN_MASKS_<t>, 1 where the spaxel’s S/N over the rest-frame window is above it, and the continuous map SNMAP. fit_sn_threshold chooses which mask the fit reads; Fitting a datacube lists the S/N methods.

Run the stages without fitting#

p = brainsp.CubePipeline("cube.fits", redshift=0.02, butterworth=True,
                         sn_mask=[3, 10], fit=False, output_dir="prepared")
p.run()

fit=False runs every enabled stage and writes their extensions, without fitting: a way to look at a prepared cube, or its S/N map, before spending time on the fit. On the command line, brainsp cube ... --no-fit does the same.

from astropy.io import fits
with fits.open("prepared/cube-BRAIN_MEGACUBE.fits") as h:
    print([x.name for x in h])
