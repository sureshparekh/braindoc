Reading a spectrum’s results#

A spectrum’s fit is a FitResult, read from the files the fit wrote. fit_spectrum() returns one, and read_result() reads one back later, from any fit, made in Python, on the command line or in the interface.

import brainsp

r = brainsp.read_result("brain_out/mock_star_forming_lines_summary.txt")
r.av, r.av_err
r.mean_log_age_light, r.mean_metallicity_light
r.lines["Halpha_6563"]

The uncertainties#

Attribute

Meaning

av_err, yav_err, v0_err, sigma_err, sigma_star_err, v_gas_err, sigma_gas_err

the 1σ errors of the headline numbers

uncertainties

every error the fit wrote, by the summary’s name: {"AV_min": 0.057, "Mage_L": 0.17, "sigma_gas group 0": 0.26, ...}

uncertainty_method

"formal" or "bootstrap"

An error is NaN where BRAIN withholds it (a formal error of a quantity only the bootstrap calibrates, or of a poorly fitted spectrum) and None where the fit wrote none. Uncertainties explains both.

r.uncertainty_method                     # 'formal'
r.uncertainties["Mage_L"]                # light-weighted mean age's error, dex

The emission lines#

r.lines["Hbeta_4861"]                    # (flux, error), as the summary writes them
r.line_fluxes()                          # every line, in the input spectrum's units × Å
r.line_fluxes(observed=True)             # ... without the stellar-extinction correction

lines holds the summary’s values: fluxes in units of the normalised spectrum times Å, corrected for the stellar extinction \(A_V\) relative to the normalisation wavelength. line_fluxes(units="input") multiplies them by fobs_norm into the input spectrum’s units, and observed=True undoes the extinction correction, giving the flux as it arrived. The tied doublets appear as "[O III]_Doublet" and "[N II]_Doublet" (the strong line’s flux) and under each member’s own name, at the fixed atomic ratio.

The population vector#

r.populations is a structured array, one row per template:

Field

Meaning

Z

metallicity

fraction of the initial mass still in stars

p = r.populations
young = p[p["age"] < 1e8]["x"].sum()          # light from stars younger than 100 Myr, per cent
old = p[p["age"] > 5e9]["mcor"].sum()         # present mass older than 5 Gyr, per cent

The spectra#

r.wavelength, r.observed, r.model, r.stellar, r.emission, r.error and r.residual are the columns of the fitted-spectrum file, as arrays on the fitted wavelengths.

Everything else#

r.values holds every value [label] line of the summary, as text, under its label up to the first bracket; anything the summary writes can be read from it. r.summary_file and r.fitted_file are the paths, r.spectrum the input. r.plot(path) writes the standard figure, and r.error_message is set for a spectrum that failed within fit_spectra().
