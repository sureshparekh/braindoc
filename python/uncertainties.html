Uncertainties#

Every quantity BRAIN reports has a 1σ uncertainty, in every mode, for spectra and for datacubes. They are made in one of two ways, chosen with the setting error_samples, and each way is trusted only for the quantities on which it has been calibrated: where a method is known to give a wrong error, BRAIN writes NaN instead.

Choose the method#

error_samples

Method

Cost

Use it for

0 (default)

formal: the fit’s own covariance, propagated to every quantity

none

\(A_V\), \(\sigma_\star\), \(v_\star\), light-weighted age and metallicity, gas kinematics, line fluxes and the line-based nebular quantities, in spectra fitted down to their noise

N (40 to 100)

bootstrap: the whole fit repeated N times on resampled spectra

N fits

everything, including age bins, mass-weighted means, masses, the nebular fraction; and every quantity of a spectrum the templates cannot fit down to its noise

 
r = brainsp.fit_spectrum("galaxy.spec", error_samples=0)       # formal
r = brainsp.fit_spectrum("galaxy.spec", error_samples=60)      # bootstrap
cube = brainsp.fit_cube("cube.fits", 0.02, error_samples=60)  # every spaxel

In the interface: Error samples in the Fit quality panel of the model.

Read them#

r.av, r.av_err
r.sigma_star, r.sigma_star_err
r.uncertainties              # every error, by name
r.uncertainty_method         # 'formal' or 'bootstrap'
r.lines["Halpha_6563"]       # (flux, error)

In the summary file: the ## Uncertainties (1 sigma) ## section, one err <name> line per quantity, and the Err column of the emission-line table.

cube.plane("Sigma_star error")
cube.plane("Halpha_6563  flux error")
cube.groups["Uncertainties"]          # every error map

In the FITS file: an extension <EXT>_ERR beside each map extension (PoPBins_ERR, PoPVecsL_ERR, PoPVecsM_ERR, PoPVecsMini_ERR, KINEMATICS_ERR, NEBMAPS_ERR, LSFCORR_ERR, and FLUX_M_ERR for the lines), plane for plane, with ERRMETH naming the method.

What the formal errors cover#

The formal errors linearise the fit at its solution. That is accurate for the parameters the fit moves smoothly, but blind to a template entering or leaving the mixture, which is how sums over templates change. BRAIN therefore writes formal errors only for:

Formal error written

stars

\(A_V\), \(Y_V\), \(\sigma_\star\) (observed and intrinsic), \(v_\star\), light-weighted \(\langle\log t\rangle\) and \(\langle Z\rangle\)

gas

the velocity and dispersion of every group; every line flux

nespop

\(A_V^{\rm neb}\), H\(\alpha\)/H\(\beta\), \(T_e\), \(n_e\), \(Q(\mathrm H)\), H\(\beta\) predicted and measured

and NaN, with a note in the file, for age bins, individual \(x_j\), mass-weighted means, masses, star formation rates, \(y_{\rm neb}\) and \(\tau\). Use the bootstrap for those.

Only where the fit reaches the noise. Where \(\chi^2\) per degree of freedom exceeds 2, the misfit is the templates’, correlated from pixel to pixel, and no factor on a covariance describes it. BRAIN then writes no formal error at all for that spectrum or spaxel, and the log says how many were affected. In practice, high-S/N spectra and the bright spaxels of a cube need the bootstrap.

How well they work#

Both methods were tested on 60 noise draws of each of three mock spectra per mode, built independently of BRAIN: the scatter of the 60 fitted values divided by the median error BRAIN reported. A ratio of 1 is right; above 1, the errors are too small.

Two limits remain, whichever method you use:

  • An age bin empty in the best fit gets too small an error even from the bootstrap, because resamples drawn around a model with no light there rarely put light there. Read it as a lower limit.

  • The bootstrap’s line-based errors (line fluxes, H\(\alpha\)/H\(\beta\), \(A_V^{\rm neb}\), \(Q\)) can be up to 1.45 times too small at moderate S/N, because a strong line is fitted by a few pixels whose residuals are smaller than their noise. Prefer the formal errors for these, where they are written.

Uncertainties gives the mathematics of both methods.

Report averages, not single templates#

Age, metallicity and dust trade against one another: an older, metal-poorer population looks like a younger, metal-richer one, and a little more dust like a little more age. Individual light fractions \(x_j\) are therefore poorly determined, while averages over them (the mean ages and metallicities, the light in broad age bins) are robust. Report those, with their bootstrap errors.
