3. An AGN continuum#

An active nucleus adds a featureless continuum, roughly a power law \(F_\nu \propto \nu^{-\alpha}\), which no stellar population can reproduce. A fit without it fakes the blue part of that continuum with young stars and the red part with dust. mock_seyfert_agn.spec is an old host with a power law (\(\alpha = 1.5\)) carrying 25 per cent of the light at 5500 Å, and Seyfert emission lines.

include_power_law=True adds seven featureless templates to whichever library is used: power laws with \(\alpha\) = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 and Cloudy’s AGN incident continuum for big-bump temperatures of \(1\), \(1.5\) and \(2 \times 10^5\) K. See AGN continuum.

"""Tutorial 3: an AGN continuum."""
import json
import brainsp

spectrum = "examples/spectra/mock_seyfert_agn.spec"
truth = json.load(open(spectrum.replace(".spec", ".truth.json")))

# Without the AGN templates the power law has to be faked with young stars.
plain = brainsp.fit_spectrum(spectrum, output_dir="fits/no_agn", quiet=True,
                             nebular_mode="espop", wavelength_scale="vacuum")
# With them: seven featureless continua (four power laws, three Cloudy AGN).
agn = brainsp.fit_spectrum(spectrum, output_dir="fits/agn", quiet=True,
                           nebular_mode="espop", wavelength_scale="vacuum",
                           include_power_law=True)
print("                     no AGN templates   with AGN templates   truth")
print(f"AGN light fraction  {plain.agn_fraction:12.1f} %   "
      f"{agn.agn_fraction:12.1f} %     {100 * truth['agn_fraction']:.0f} %")
print(f"<log age>_L         {plain.mean_log_age_light:12.2f}     "
      f"{agn.mean_log_age_light:12.2f}       "
      f"{truth['mean_log_age_light']:.2f}")
print(f"A_V                 {plain.av:12.3f}     {agn.av:12.3f}       "
      f"{truth['av']:.2f}")
print(f"chi2/N              {plain.chi2:12.3f}     {agn.chi2:12.3f}")

young = plain.populations
print("\nlight in populations younger than 100 Myr:",
      f"{young[(young['age'] < 1e8) & young['stellar']]['x'].sum():.1f} % "
      "without AGN templates,",
      f"{agn.populations[(agn.populations['age'] < 1e8) & agn.populations['stellar']]['x'].sum():.1f} % with")

print("\nAGN components used:")
for row in agn.populations[~agn.populations["stellar"]]:
    if row["x"] > 0:
        print(f"  {row['name']:22s} {row['x']:6.2f} %")
brainsp.plots.sfh(plain, "t03_sfh_no_agn.png", kind="light")
brainsp.plots.sfh(agn, "t03_sfh_agn.png", kind="light")
brainsp.plots.spectrum(agn, "t03_spectrum.png")

Without the AGN templates the fit invents 7.4 per cent of the light in populations younger than 100 Myr and makes the mean age 0.4 dex too young and \(A_V\) 0.22 mag too high. With them, the AGN takes 24.3 per cent (truth 25), the young population disappears, and age and extinction come back to the truth.

Only the sum of the AGN components is a measurement: in the optical the Cloudy continua are power laws of slope 0.59 to 0.74 to within 1.4 per cent, so how the light is split between neighbouring AGN templates is set by noise. r.agn_fraction is that sum.
