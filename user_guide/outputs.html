Output files#

A spectrum#

Every fit of a spectrum writes, into the output folder (brain_out by default):

File

What

<name>_summary.txt

the fitted parameters, the population vector, the emission lines

<name>_fitted.txt

the observed and model spectra, column by column

<name>_settings.txt

every setting the fit used, and the templates

<name>_fit.pdf

the standard figure (when make_plot is on)

From Python, brainsp.FitResult reads the first two (Working with results in Python): brainsp.read_result("<name>_summary.txt").

Every fit writes two text files into the output folder.

The summary file#

*_summary.txt is plain text: a value, then the key in square brackets.

Fit quality#

  5.58653E-01                             [chi2/Nl_eff]
     20.45878                             [adev (%)]

Key

Meaning

chi2/Nl_eff

\(\chi^2\) divided by the number of fitted pixels, nothing subtracted for the free parameters. Near 1 is a good fit; well below 1 usually means the errors are overestimated. The cube’s CHI2 extension carries this same quantity, and its CHI2RED extension the reduced \(\chi^2\)

adev

Mean absolute deviation between model and data, as a percentage

Totals#

  7.01277E-02                             [Flux_tot (units of input spectrum!)]
  4.10394E+03                             [Mini_tot (apparent; x flux_unit * 4*pi*d_cm^2 / 3.828E33 for Msun)]
  2.86073E+03                             [Mcor_tot (apparent; x flux_unit * 4*pi*d_cm^2 / 3.828E33 for Msun)]

Key

Meaning

Flux_tot

the model’s flux density at the normalisation wavelength, in the input spectrum’s units: \(\sum_j x_j\) times fobs_norm

fobs_norm

the observed flux density at the normalisation wavelength (median of ±10 pixels), in the input spectrum’s units: the factor the spectrum was divided by, and what turns a normalised flux back into the input’s units

Mini_tot

mass initially formed, over the stellar populations

Mcor_tot

mass still in stars (and remnants) today

The masses are apparent: the observed flux divided by the library’s flux per unit mass. BRAIN never uses a distance, so to turn them into solar masses multiply by your flux unit (in erg/s/cm²/Å) times \(4\pi d^2\) (cm²) and divide by \(L_\odot = 3.828\times10^{33}\) erg/s, as the label says.

Both totals run over the stellar populations only. A featureless power-law component contributes light and no mass, so it is in Flux_tot and in x_j and in neither total; see the population table below.

Mcor_tot is always the smaller of the two. Use it when comparing against a dynamical mass; use Mini_tot when comparing against an integrated star-formation history.

The two dispersions in this file follow different conventions

Every line carrying a dispersion says which it is. vd_min, sigma_obs and every sigma_gas are OBSERVED: the instrument is still in them. sigma_star is INTRINSIC: the instrumental line-spread function has been taken out in quadrature. An observed and an intrinsic dispersion from the same file are not comparable as printed, which is why the label states the convention instead of leaving it to the name.

The instrumental line-spread function is not yet applied to the emission line, so sigma_gas is an observed dispersion today. sigma_inst is printed beside it so that the subtraction is available; see below for why BRAIN does not do it for you.

Key

Meaning

sigma_obs

The same number as vd_min. On its own it is a statement about the templates, not about the galaxy

delta_sigma_inst

Signed quadrature difference between the two line-spread functions as the library entered the fit. Positive means the data is broader. A group that was pre-convolved contributes zero

sigma_star

$\mathrm{sign}(x)\sqrt{

With instrumental_resolution blank, the last two read unknown and vd_min is an observed dispersion that includes the instrument.

< value means an upper limit

A sigma_star written with a leading < is not a measurement. Either the fit came back sitting on the lower bound of dispersion_range, or the broadening the two line-spread functions alone demand already exceeds what the fit applied. A comment block under the table says which, with numbers.

The population table#

One row per template:

# j    x_j(%)    Mini_j(%)   Mcor_j(%)   age_j(yr)     Z_j     (L/M)_j  YAV?  Mstars  component_j
  1   21.0944   0.0000E+00  0.0000E+00  1.000000E+00  0.00000  9.787E+00  0   1.0000  Power_150
  2    0.0000   0.0000E+00  0.0000E+00  1.000000E+06  0.00370  3.171E-03  0   1.0000  ageGe_00_z004

Column

Meaning

x_j

Light fraction at the normalisation wavelength, in per cent

Mini_j

Initial mass fraction

Mcor_j

Surviving mass fraction

age_j, Z_j

Age in years and metallicity

(L/M)_j

Light-to-mass ratio for that population

Mstars

Surviving stellar mass fraction at that age

Light fractions are not mass fractions

A 1 Myr population can carry 20% of the light and a millionth of the mass. Young stars are enormously more luminous per unit mass. Always be explicit about which fraction you are quoting.

The power law carries light and no mass

The row above is the AGN power law that include_power_law adds. Its x_j is whatever light it took — 21% in this fit — and its Mini_j and Mcor_j are exactly zero. That is the correct output and not a failed component: a power law has no stars, so there is no initial mass, no surviving fraction and nothing for Mstars to mean. Its (L/M)_j is the flux of the file it was generated from at the normalisation wavelength, which is an amplitude somebody chose, not a light-to-mass ratio.

So the two sets of percentages are normalised over different components. x_j runs over the whole base and adds to 100. Mini_j and Mcor_j run over the stellar populations alone and add to 100 over those. A component that is 21% of the light and 0% of the mass is reading exactly right.

Most rows will be exactly zero. That is expected and correct: the solver enforces non-negativity and finds a sparse solution, typically 10–20 non-zero populations out of 84. It is not a failure to converge.

Gas kinematics#

Under the population table, one block per kinematic group:

## Gas Kinematics ##
         57.30                            [sigma_gas group 0 (km/s), OBSERVED]
         30.85                            [v0_gas group 0 (km/s)]
       unknown                            [sigma_inst group 0 (km/s), needs instrumental_resolution]
          5/10                            [lines carrying flux in group 0]
        <58.39                            [sigma_gas group 1 (km/s), OBSERVED, UPPER LIMIT]
        353.12                            [v0_gas group 1 (km/s)]
       unknown                            [sigma_inst group 1 (km/s), needs instrumental_resolution]
           2/4                            [lines carrying flux in group 1]
# group 1 came back on the lower bound of gas_dispersion_range ...

Key

Meaning

sigma_gas

The dispersion fitted for that group’s lines, observed

v0_gas

The velocity fitted for that group’s lines

sigma_inst

The instrumental dispersion at that group’s lines, flux-weighted, or unknown when instrumental_resolution is not set

lines carrying flux

How many of the group’s lines came back with non-zero flux

The block is absent from a spop fit, which fits no lines.

<, > and unconstrained are not numbers

These read exactly as sigma_star does, and for the same reason.

  • <value — the fit came back on the lower end of gas_dispersion_range. It wanted a narrower line than it was allowed, so the value is an upper limit and not a width.

  • >value — the same at the upper end: a lower limit.

  • unconstrained — no line in that group has any flux. All of them came back at exactly zero from the non-negative solve, so nothing in the spectrum constrains either the dispersion or the velocity of that group. The optimiser still returns numbers and still reports success; they are artefacts of where the search started. What it returned is in the # comment underneath, where it cannot be mistaken for a result.

A comment block under each group says which applies, with numbers.

BRAIN does not subtract the instrument from the gas, on purpose

sigma_gas and sigma_inst are reported separately and nothing is clipped, floored or called zero when the first comes out below the second. That follows Westfall et al. (2019, §7.3), who report the observed and the instrumental dispersion as separate maps and leave the corrected velocity dispersion to the user. A measured dispersion below the instrumental one is not a measurement of \(\sigma = 0\) and must not be clipped to one (§7.6.3); the recommendation there is to carry the negative \(\sigma^2\) through rather than throw it away. Both numbers are in the file so that you can.

Emission-line fluxes#

## Emission Lines ##
 vacuum                                   [wavelength_scale of the OBSERVED spectrum; ...]
# Fluxes are in units of the NORMALISED spectrum times Angstrom: multiply by fobs_norm ...
# They are corrected for the stellar extinction AV_min, relative to the normalisation wavelength: ...
# A tied doublet's flux is its strong line's; the weak line is fixed at the atomic ratio.
Line/Doublet                        |         Flux |        Err
[O II]_3726                         |   1.5283e+00 | 0.0000e+00

Two conventions apply to these numbers, and the file states both:

  1. Normalised units. The spectrum is fitted after dividing it by its flux at the normalisation wavelength (normalisation_wavelength, 5500 Å), so a line flux of 6 is six times the continuum flux density at 5500 Å times one Ångström. Multiply by fobs_norm for the input spectrum’s units.

  2. Corrected for the stellar extinction. The model reddens the lines with the stars’ \(A_V\), relative to the normalisation wavelength, so the fitted amplitude is the flux before that dust: multiply by \(10^{-0.4 A_V [q(\lambda) - q(\lambda_0)]}\) for the flux as observed.

brainsp.FitResult.line_fluxes() does both: units="input" and observed=True. The Err column is zero unless error_samples is set. A datacube’s line maps are already observed fluxes in the cube’s units (below).

Gas diagnostics#

In nepop and photopop mode the summary carries extra measurements:

Quantity

From

Electron density \(n_e\)

[S II] λ6716/λ6731 ratio

[O III] (λ4959+λ5007)/λ4363 ratio

Balmer decrement

Hα/Hβ, from the dust-attenuated line fluxes

The Balmer decrement against Case B in nepop mode; the value that minimises \(\chi^2\) in photopop mode

BPT classification

Star-forming, composite, AGN or undetermined

Line fluxes are listed underneath, as Line/Doublet | Flux | Err, under a heading that states the wavelength scale the lines were placed on:

## Emission Lines ##
 vacuum                                   [wavelength_scale of the OBSERVED spectrum; the rest wavelengths these lines were placed at are on it]
Line/Doublet                        |         Flux |        Err

That line is a copy of the wavelength_scale setting, not a measurement. It is in the file because air and vacuum rest wavelengths differ by about 83 km/s and nothing else in the output would reveal which was used; a flux table that does not say is a table whose gas velocity is ambiguous by 83 km/s. The same declaration reaches a megacube as the BRAINWSC header card. BRAIN does not currently compute equivalent widths — line_fitter.py, which used to, was removed; if you need EWs, measure them from *_fitted.txt yourself.

Only lines that were actually fitted are listed

A line is fitted only if it can land on your wavelength grid at some velocity and dispersion inside gas_velocity_range and gas_dispersion_range. One that cannot is dropped before the fit and named in the log:

2 emission line(s) cannot reach the 4800-5100 A grid at any velocity or
dispersion the fit is allowed, and are NOT fitted or reported:
Hgamma_4340 (4340 A), [O III]_4363 (4363 A)

It then does not appear in the flux table. A flux of 0.0000e+00 in that table therefore means a genuine non-detection — the line was fitted and the best fit gave it no flux — and never “the line was off the end of the spectrum”. Those two used to be indistinguishable.

A doublet that lost one component is renamed

If a tied doublet has one component on the grid and one off, the survivor is fitted alone, its ratio is set to 1.0, and the entry takes that component’s own name — [O III]_5007 rather than [O III]_Doublet. The listed flux is then the flux of that one line. The log says so:

[O III]_Doublet loses [O III]_4959 to the edge of the 4980-5300 A grid;
the remaining [O III]_5007 is fitted alone, at ratio 1.0, and reported
as '[O III]_5007'

In photopop mode four further lines say how well \(A_{V,\rm neb}\) is pinned down. Read them before quoting it:

A datacube: the megacube#

Written to <save_path_root>/<stem>-BRAIN_MEGACUBE.fits, with any -RAW removed from the stem. The input extensions come through unchanged, then:

The compatibility contract#

Six extensions, spelled exactly like this, are what existing analysis tools read. The test suite fits a small cube and asserts all six.

Extension

Shape

Contents

BaseAgeMetal

(n_base, 2)

column 0 age in years, column 1 Z as a mass fraction

PoPBins

(n_map, y, x)

the map stack, planes named by DATA<n> cards

PoPVecsL

(n_base, y, x)

light fraction per template, per cent

PoPVecsM

(n_base, y, x)

present-mass fraction per template, per cent

(nλ, y, x)

(nλ, y, x)

FLXOBS and FLXSYN carry CRPIX3, CRVAL3, CD3_3 and WAT3_001, because the viewers build their wavelength axis as np.arange(CRVAL3, CD3_3*n + CRVAL3, CD3_3) and have no fallback. The step is flxobs_step, 1 Å by default, which is the published value.

This is the only place BRAIN resamples an observed flux

And only for the output copy, never for the fit, which runs on its own log grid. WAVE is written as well, so nothing has to trust the WCS, and STELLAR and MODEL stay on the cube’s own wavelength grid, where no resolution is thrown away.

Every extension also carries the BRAIN* provenance cards, among them BRAINZ (the redshift removed), BRAINFRM (the frame fitted in) and BRAINWSC (air or vacuum, the wavelength scale of the observed spectrum that the emission-line rest wavelengths were placed on). A finished cube says which scale its gas velocities are measured against; the two differ by about 83 km/s and nothing else in the file would reveal it.

PoPBins#

One 3D extension whose planes are addressed by header cards: DATA7 holds the name of plane 7, and the card’s comment describes it. In order:

FC<exp> (only when include_power_law is on) · <bin>_light · <bin>_mass · Av · Mage_L · Mage_M · MZ_L · MZ_M · F_Norm · Sigma_star · vrot_star · Adev · ChiSqrt · SNR · then BRAIN’s own: YAV · Nl_eff · Niter · BadSpaxel · Sigma_gas_g<n> · vrot_gas_g<n> · Sigma_star_c · Dsigma_inst · LSF_flag.

The tail is appended after SNR so that a reader indexing the stack by position still finds everything it knew about where it was.

Mage_L is the light-weighted mean of log₁₀(age/yr), not the logarithm of the mean age. MZ_L is the weighted mean of Z as a mass fraction, not of [Z/H]. As in the published megacubes (Riffel et al. 2023), both sum over the stellar templates but divide by the light of every component, featureless ones included, so AGN light pulls them down: 10 per cent of it lowers Mage_L by about 0.94 dex. For the mean of the stars alone, divide by the stellar fraction, (100 − AGN_light)/100. Mage_M and MZ_M are unaffected, since a featureless component has no mass. Sigma_star is an observed dispersion: it includes the instrument unless instrumental_resolution is set, in which case Sigma_star_c is the corrected one and LSF_flag says whether that is a measurement, an upper limit, or not reportable.

An unfitted spaxel is 0, not blank

That is what the published megacubes carry and what their viewers scale their colour bars to. Since 0 is a plausible value for most of these quantities, the stack carries a BadSpaxel plane of its own, 1 wherever the spaxel is not a converged fit — including the spaxels nothing was ever attempted on — and the file carries BADSPXL and STATUS. A map value is a measurement only where BadSpaxel is 0. A spaxel whose fit did not converge is flagged there too: its maps are the last state of an optimisation that had not finished.

Masses, star-formation rates and grouped AGN components#

BRAIN’s masses are apparent: the observed flux divided by the library’s flux per unit mass. With the galaxy’s distance (galaxy_distance, Mpc; per cube, a galaxy distance column of the targets table) they become solar masses, multiplied by flux_unit × 4πd² / L_sun (L☉ = 3.826×10³³ erg/s and 1 Mpc = 3.08567758×10²⁴ cm, the published megacubes’ values; the flux unit’s number is read from BUNIT, e.g. 10⁻¹⁷ for MaNGA, or given as flux_scale). PoPBins then also carries

Plane

Contents

Mstar

present mass in stars, M☉

Mpcross

mass processed into stars (initial mass), M☉

<name> from sfr_bins

star-formation rate, M☉/yr: Mstar × (initial-mass fraction with min < age ≤ max) / (max − min)

Without a distance none of these is written: the missing factor is about 3.5 dex for a MaNGA spaxel and distance-dependent. The mass fractions need no distance and are always in PoPVecsM and the <bin>_mass planes.

The SFR follows the published megacubes’ definition: the initial-mass fraction in the bin times the present stellar mass. The initial mass actually formed in the bin is larger by Mpcross / Mstar, typically 1.6.

fc_bins and bb_bins group the featureless AGN components by the power-law exponent or blackbody temperature their names give (FC0.25, BB800), each [name, min, max], min exclusive, max inclusive, in per cent of all the light: for example FC_Tot (0.245, 0.75] or BB_warm (699, 1000]. Population bins may overlap, so totals such as xy_Tot are bins of their own.

Population age bins#

population_bins is a list of [name, youngest, oldest] in years. Each bin becomes two planes, <name>_light and <name>_mass. A template belongs to a bin when min < age ≤ max — min exclusive, max inclusive, which is the published convention and matters because the standard bin sets put edges exactly on template ages.

The default is the six MaNGA bins, with the edges published MaNGA megacubes use, so a BRAIN map and one of those can be compared without re-binning either:

Bins may leave gaps — these do — and the fit logs how much light falls into them. Overlapping bins are refused: a template inside two bins is counted twice and every map still looks reasonable afterwards. A power-law component is never in any bin; it is reported on its own FC card.

χ² convention — two extensions, named

CHI2 and PoPBins:ChiSqrt both carry χ² / Nl,eff, where Nl,eff is the number of pixels of non-zero weight that entered the χ². That is what the single-spectrum summary file reports on its first line, and what the published megacubes report, so a spaxel of a BRAIN cube and the same spectrum fitted on its own quote the same number, computed by the same function.

CHI2RED carries χ² / (Nl,eff − npar), the reduced χ², with npar = 4 + 2 × ngroups free non-linear parameters — written into that extension’s NPAR card. Each extension states its denominator in a DENOM card as well, so neither convention can be assumed silently.

The two differ by Nl,eff/(Nl,eff − npar): a fraction of a per cent on a well-sampled spaxel, several per cent on a heavily masked one. CHI2 held the reduced convention in cubes written before this change; those files carry no CHI2RED extension and no DENOM card, which is how to tell them apart.
