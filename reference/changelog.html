Changes#

The current release (in preparation)#

BRAIN becomes the installable Python package brainsp, with a desktop interface, a command line and a library that share one engine and one set of settings.

Science#

  • Native pixels. The model is built on a fine logarithmic grid and integrated into the data’s own pixels; the data are never resampled. Resampling biased gas dispersions by +2 to +10 km s⁻¹ and made \(\chi^2/N\) fall to 0.65–0.75 for a perfect model (Native pixels).

  • The instrument in the line width. Each emission line’s width is the gas’s own dispersion and the instrument’s in quadrature, so sigma_gas is intrinsic when the resolution is known. A MaNGA cube’s own line-spread function (PRESPECRES) is read automatically.

  • The fit as a sequence (FADO’s): stellar kinematics from the stars alone, the joint fit for the gas kinematics, the populations on the line-free pixels, every line on the data minus the continuum.

  • nespop: the nebular continuum of the populations’ own ionising photons, Case B, \(T_e\) and \(n_e\) measured on dereddened fluxes, \(A_V^{\rm neb}\) from the Balmer decrement, and H\(\alpha\) and H\(\beta\) tied to the photons where the gas is ionised by stars (FC). Validated with BC03; refused for a library that does not reach the Lyman limit, and run with a warning for one not yet validated (M13). The former photopop and nepop modes are folded into it.

  • An uncertainty for every quantity, in every mode: formal errors (the fit’s sandwich covariance) where calibrated on noise draws and where the fit reaches its noise, and the wild bootstrap of the whole fit (error_samples) for everything (Uncertainties).

  • \(A_V\) from −1 by default (dust_range: [-1, 5]).

Speed and machines#

  • The CPU gives the GPU’s results. On a CPU every stage of a datacube fit now runs, as on a GPU; before, the worker path skipped the stars-alone kinematics, the weak lines, the masked population fit and the line errors.

  • A CPU worker pool for every stage, one worker per physical core, never more than all but two cores, kept warm from one cube to the next. A 180-spaxel espop cube: 89 s on 32 CPU cores, 90 s on an RTX 3060.

  • GPU sizing from the card: about two lanes per streaming multiprocessor, within the card’s memory, in place of a fixed cap of 64.

  • Many spectra fitted together: spectra that share settings, wavelengths and usable pixels are fitted as one cube (six spectra: 65 s to 16 s on a GPU), with every number as fitted alone.

  • Several cubes at once, and one cube over several GPUs (gpu_slots), with the megacubes of a sequential run.

  • espop on GPUs about 9 times faster than in the first BRAIN (MaNGA 8626-12704: 1395 s to 158 s).

Python, command line and interface#

  • The Python library: Settings, fit_spectrum(), fit_spectra(), FitResult, CubePipeline, fit_cube(), Megacube, brainsp.plots, brainsp.libraries, brainsp.mock, write_script(), selftest(). Everything the interface does can be done from code, including every map with its error ("<map> error"), the nebular maps, FitResult.uncertainties and FitResult.nebular, masks given as regions, and the pre-fitting stages alone (fit=False).

  • The command line: brainsp cube gains --spectral-bin, --degrade, --butterworth, --law, --no-fit and --gpu-slots.

  • The interface: the fitting mode first in the model, choosing BC03 for nespop and greying out libraries that cannot run it; every step marked Required or Optional; a light and a dark theme; the Results workspace with every map, its error, the nebular maps and the diagnostics.

  • Template libraries found by name; BasesGM, M13 and seven AGN continua ship with the package, and more are added with brainsp libraries install.

  • Mock data with known answers, and a self-test that fits them.

  • This documentation, rewritten.
