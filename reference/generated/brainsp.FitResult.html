FitResult#

class brainsp.FitResult(summary_file, fitted_file=None, spectrum=None)[source]#

Bases: object

The result of fitting one spectrum, read from the files BRAIN wrote.

A fit writes two files: <name>_summary.txt (the fitted parameters, the population vector, the emission lines) and <name>_fitted.txt (the observed and model spectra, column by column). This object reads both.

Attributes:
spectrumstr

The input spectrum.

summary_file, fitted_filestr

The two output files.

valuesdict

Every value [label] line of the summary, by label, as text.

v0, sigmafloat

Stellar velocity and velocity dispersion in km/s (v0_min, vd_min; the dispersion is OBSERVED, including the instrument, unless instrumental_resolution was given; see sigma_star).

sigma_starfloat or None

The intrinsic stellar dispersion, when the instrumental resolution was known.

av, yavfloat

V-band extinction of the whole population and the extra extinction of the youngest components, in magnitudes.

chi2, chi2_data, adevfloat

Reduced chi-squared (with and without the penalty rows), and the mean absolute deviation in per cent.

populationsnumpy.ndarray

Structured array, one row per template: x (light fraction, per cent, at the normalisation wavelength), mini and mcor (initial and present mass fractions, per cent), age (yr), Z, mstars, name, stellar (False for an AGN component).

linesdict

Emission-line fluxes and errors, {name: (flux, error)}, as the summary writes them: in units of the normalised spectrum times Angstrom, corrected for the stellar extinction (line_fluxes() converts them); empty in spop. Names are BRAIN’s ("Halpha_6563", "[S II]_6716" …). The tied doublets appear as "[O III]_Doublet" and "[N II]_Doublet" (the strong line’s flux) and also under each member’s own name, at the fixed ratio.

wavelength, observed, model, stellar, emission, error, residualndarray

The columns of the fitted-spectrum file.

uncertaintiesdict

Every 1-sigma error the fit wrote, by the summary’s name of the quantity: {"AV_min": 0.021, "sigma_star": 3.4, "Mage_L": ...}.

nebulardict

nespop’s nebular results, from the summary’s photoionisation block:

Examples

>>> r = brainsp.fit_spectrum("galaxy.spec")
>>> r.v0, r.sigma, r.av
(188.3, 47.55, 0.2171)
>>> r.mean_log_age_light, r.mean_log_age_mass
>>> young = r.populations[r.populations["age"] < 1e8]["x"].sum()
>>> r.plot("galaxy_fit.pdf")

property uncertainties#

Every 1-sigma error the fit wrote, by the summary’s name of the quantity: {"AV_min": 0.021, "sigma_star": 3.4, "Mage_L": ...}. NaN where the error is withheld (formal errors of a poor fit, or of a quantity only the bootstrap calibrates); empty when the fit wrote none. uncertainty_method says how they were made.

line_fluxes(units='input', observed=False)[source]#

Emission-line fluxes, in the units asked for.

The summary file holds fluxes relative to the normalised spectrum (continuum = 1 at the normalisation wavelength) and corrected for the stellar extinction relative to that wavelength. This converts them.

Parameters:
units{“input”, “normalised”}

"input" multiplies by fobs_norm, giving flux in the units of the input spectrum times Angstrom; "normalised" leaves them as written.

observedbool

Undo the stellar-extinction correction, giving the flux as it arrives (still after any Galactic dereddening of the input).

Returns:
dict

{name: (flux, error)}, doublets and their members included.
