fit_spectra#

brainsp.fit_spectra(spectra, settings=None, *, corrections=None, output_dir=None, device=None, quiet=False, stop_on_error=False, workers=None, **overrides)[source]#

Fit several spectra, each with its own pre-fit corrections if given.

Spectra the fit would treat alike (the same settings, the same wavelengths once corrected, the same usable pixels) are fitted together, as one cube of N spectra: on a GPU every lane of the queue is then busy, and on a CPU the worker pool takes them. Each spectrum gets the result it would get fitted alone, to rounding. The others are fitted one at a time, on CPU worker processes when there is no GPU. Templates and the compiled engine are loaded once and reused.

Parameters:
spectrastr or list of str

Files, or a glob pattern such as "spectra/*.spec". May be omitted (None) when corrections is a table: its spectra are fitted.

settingsSettings, dict or str, optional

Settings shared by every spectrum.

correctionsstr or dict, optional

A CSV table (see read_corrections_table()) or a mapping {spectrum: {setting: value}} of per-spectrum settings, typically spectrum_redshift, spectrum_in_rest_frame, galactic_ebv, galactic_rv, galactic_law.

output_dir, device, quiet, **overrides

As for fit_spectrum().

stop_on_errorbool

Raise on the first failure. Otherwise a failed spectrum gives a result whose error_message says why, and the batch carries on.

workersint or “auto”, optional

On a CPU, fit the spectra on this many worker processes at once, each with one thread and each keeping its own compiled fit warm. "auto" (the default on a CPU) uses one per physical core, never more than every core but two, as many as the free memory allows; 1 fits them all in this process. On a GPU the spectra that cannot share a batch are fitted one after another in this process.

Returns:
list of FitResult

In the order the spectra were given.
