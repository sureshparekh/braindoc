Spectrum output files#

Every fit of a spectrum writes, to the output folder (brain_out by default), files named after the spectrum:

File

Content

<name>_summary.txt

the results, described below

<name>_fitted.txt

the observed and model spectra, column by column

<name>_settings.txt

every setting the fit used and the templates it used

<name>_mask.sm

the mask, when it was given as regions

<name>_fit.pdf

the standard figure, when make_plot is on

brainsp.FitResult reads the first two: brainsp.read_result("<name>_summary.txt").

The summary file#

Plain text, one value per line followed by its label in square brackets, with # lines as comments and ## lines as section headings. A label states the unit and, for every dispersion, the convention.

Input#

## Some input info
/data/galaxy.spec                         [arq_obs]
BasesGM                                   [arq_base]
None                                      [arq_masks]
84                                        [N_base]
CCM                                       [red_law_option]
5500                                      [normalisation_wavelength (A)]
espop                                     [photoionization_mode]
0                                         [E(B-V) Galactic, removed before fitting]

Fit quality and totals#

Label

Meaning

chi2/Nl_eff

\(\chi^2\) over the \(N_{l,\rm eff}\) pixels fitted (data, regularisation and Case B rows); near 1 is a good fit, well below 1 usually means overestimated errors

chi2_data/Nl_eff

the same over the data rows only

adev

the mean absolute deviation between model and data, per cent

Flux_tot

the model’s flux at \(\lambda_0\), in the input units: \(\sum_j x_j\) times fobs_norm

fobs_norm

the observed flux the spectrum was divided by, in the input units

Mini_tot, Mcor_tot

the initial and present stellar mass, apparent: multiply by the flux unit × \(4\pi d^2\) / 3.828 × 10³³ for M☉

Stellar parameters#

Label

Meaning

v0_min

the stellar velocity, km s⁻¹

vd_min

the dispersion applied to the library, km s⁻¹, observed

sigma_obs

the same, named for what it is

delta_sigma_inst

the signed quadrature difference of the data’s and the library’s line-spread functions, flux-weighted

sigma_star

the intrinsic stellar dispersion, \(\mathrm{sign}(x)\sqrt{\lvert x\rvert}\) with \(x = \sigma_{\rm obs}^2 - \Delta\sigma_{\rm inst}^2\); unknown when a resolution is unknown; a leading < marks an upper limit

instrumental_resolution, library_resolution

the two line-spread functions and where they came from

spectrum_redshift removed before fitting

the redshift used to place the instrument’s resolution

LSF correction applied to the library, templates pre-convolved

which template groups were convolved up to the data’s resolution

AV_min, YAV_min

\(A_V\) and \(Y_V\), mag

The population table#

One row per template:

Column

Meaning

j

the template’s index

x_j(%)

light fraction at \(\lambda_0\)

Mini_j(%), Mcor_j(%)

initial and present mass fraction, over the stellar templates only

age_j(yr), Z_j

age and metallicity

(L/M)_j

the library’s flux per unit mass at \(\lambda_0\)

YAV?

1 if the template receives the young-star extinction \(Y_V\)

Mstars

the fraction of the initial mass still in stars

component_j

the template’s name

kind

ssp for a stellar population, otherwise a featureless component

Most rows are exactly zero: the solution is sparse by nature (The linear solve). The light fractions run over every component and add to 100; the mass fractions run over the stellar templates and add to 100 over those, since a featureless component carries light and no mass.

Gas kinematics (espop, nespop)#

One block per kinematic group:

## Gas Kinematics ##
         60.24       [sigma_gas group 0 (km/s), INTRINSIC, the instrument in the line model]
         29.42       [v0_gas group 0 (km/s)]
         70.73       [sigma_inst group 0 (km/s), at the group's lines, flux-weighted]
         14/14       [lines setting group 0's kinematics that are detected (S/N >= 3)]

sigma_gas is intrinsic when instrumental_resolution is known (the instrument is in the line model) and observed otherwise; the label says which. A leading < or > marks a value on a bound of gas_dispersion_range, a limit rather than a measurement, and unconstrained a group none of whose lines is detected: its kinematics mean nothing, and its lines’ fluxes are upper limits. A comment block under each group explains which applies.

Uncertainties#

## Uncertainties (1 sigma) ##
 formal                                   [err method]
  6.18486E+00                             [err v0_min (km/s)]
  5.73937E-02                             [err AV_min (mag)]
  8.94513E+00                             [Mage_L (light-weighted <log age/yr>, stars; as the cube's PoPBins)]
  1.66513E-01                             [err Mage_L (light-weighted <log age/yr>)]
          NAN                             [err Mage_M (mass-weighted <log age/yr>)]

One err <name> line per quantity, with the method on the first line. NAN means no error is given: a parameter on a bound, a formal error of a quantity only the bootstrap calibrates, or a formal error of a fit whose \(\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}\) exceeds 2 (Uncertainties). With the bootstrap, a table of each template’s light and mass errors follows.

Emission lines (espop, nespop)#

## Emission Lines ##
 vacuum                                   [wavelength_scale of the OBSERVED spectrum; ...]
Line/Doublet                        |         Flux |        Err
Hbeta_4861                          |   7.2690e+01 | 7.7975e-01
Halpha_6563                         |   2.0339e+02 | 2.0706e+00
[O III]_Doublet                     |   1.4376e+02 | 1.1666e+00

Two conventions, both stated in the file:

  1. Normalised units. The fluxes are in units of the normalised spectrum times Å: multiply by fobs_norm for the input spectrum’s units.

  2. Corrected for the stellar extinction. The model reddens the lines with the stars’ \(A_V\) relative to \(\lambda_0\), so the fitted flux is the flux before that dust; multiply by \(10^{-0.4A_V[k(\lambda) - k(\lambda_0)]}\) for the flux as observed.

brainsp.FitResult.line_fluxes() does both (units="input", observed=True). A tied doublet’s flux is its strong line’s. Err is the 1σ error: formal by default (the covariance of the line measurement at the fitted kinematics and continuum, widened by \(\sqrt{\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}}\) where that exceeds 1, with \(A_V\)’s error added) or the bootstrap’s. For a line measured at zero it is the flux a 1σ line would have, so 3 × Err is an upper limit. Only lines that can land on the fitted range are listed, so a zero flux is a real non-detection.

Photoionisation diagnostics (nespop)#

Label

Meaning

treatment

FC (H\(\alpha\), H\(\beta\) tied to the populations’ photons) or NC (nebular continuum alone)

Q_eff, Q(H)_total

the Lyman-continuum photons of the fitted populations (normalised units; apparent, as Mcor_tot)

tau

H\(\beta\) predicted from \(Q(\mathrm H)\), reddened by \(A_V^{\rm neb}\), over H\(\beta\) measured; 1 is consistent

AV_neb

the gas extinction, and whether it came from the Balmer decrement or is tied to the stellar \(A_V\)

T_e, n_e

the gas conditions, or default where they could not be measured

BPT_flag, BPT_class

the BPT class that decided FC or NC

f_neb

the nebular continuum’s fraction of the light at \(\lambda_0\)

followed by a table of the Balmer lines predicted from \(Q(\mathrm H)\) and measured.

The fitted spectrum#

<name>_fitted.txt holds one row per data pixel, on the spectrum’s own wavelengths (after the corrections and the range cut):

# Wavelength Flux_Obs Total_Fit Stellar_Fit Emission_Fit Err Resid
3.700000e+03 8.267133e-01 7.966507e-01 7.940145e-01 2.636262e-03 4.410413e-02 3.006255e-02

Column

Meaning

Wavelength

Å, in the frame the fit ran in

Flux_Obs

the observed spectrum, normalised

Total_Fit

the complete model

Stellar_Fit

the stellar part

Emission_Fit

the emission lines; zero in spop

Nebular_Fit

the nebular continuum, in nespop only (the column is absent otherwise)

Err

the error used in the fit

Resid

Flux_Obs − Total_Fit

 
import numpy as np
wl, obs, model, stars, lines, err, resid = np.loadtxt(
    "brain_out/galaxy_fitted.txt", unpack=True)        # spop and espop

FitResult reads the columns by their names, whichever the mode.
