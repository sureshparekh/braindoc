Uncertainties#

Every quantity BRAIN reports has a 1σ uncertainty, in every mode. This page gives the mathematics of the two methods that make them and the tests that decide where each is written; Uncertainties is the practical guide.

Formal errors (error_samples: 0)#

Let \(\mathbf z\) be every quantity the fit moves together: \(A_V\), \(Y_V\), \(\sigma_\star\), \(v_\star\), the gas kinematics and the weights. Let \(J\) be the derivative of the fit’s weighted residual (the data rows, the smoothness rows and, in nespop, the Case B rows, with the weights the fit used) with respect to the free entries of \(\mathbf z\), at the solution. Then

\[ C = f\,H^{-1}J_d^{\sf T}J_d\,H^{-1} \;+\; \sum_{L\in\{\rm H\alpha, H\beta\}} \sigma_{F_L}^2\,\mathbf s_L\mathbf s_L^{\sf T}, \qquad H = J^{\sf T}J, \qquad f = \max\!\Big(1, \frac{\chi^2}{N_{\rm eff} - n_{\rm free}}\Big). \]

The first term is the data’s noise carried through the fit, in the sandwich form that is correct when the fit’s own weighting includes rows that are not data (\(J_d\) is \(J\) restricted to the data rows). The second term, in nespop, is the noise of the H\(\alpha\) and H\(\beta\) fluxes measured before the population fit, which enter it twice, as the Case B targets and through \(A_V^{\rm neb}\) (the Balmer decrement made of them, which the fit holds):

\[ \mathbf s_L = \frac{d\mathbf z}{dF_L} = -H^{-1}J^{\sf T}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial F_L} + \frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial A_V^{\rm neb}}\,\frac{\partial A_V^{\rm neb}}{\partial F_L}\Big). \]

The factor \(f\) widens the errors of a fit worse than its errors allow, and never narrows them. Each reported quantity \(q\) then gets \(\sigma_q^2 = \sum_k(\nabla_{\mathbf z}q\cdot L_k)^2\), with \(C = LL^{\sf T}\), the gradient taken by central differences. A weight at zero and a parameter on a bound of its range are not free, since the fit did not find a minimum there; a parameter on a bound has no error (NaN).

Where they are written. The formal errors linearise the fit at its solution, so they cannot see a template entering or leaving it. Quantities built from sums over templates (age bins, \(x_j\), mass-weighted means, masses, star formation rates, \(y_{\rm neb}\), \(\tau\)) change in exactly that way, and their formal errors are wrong: an empty age bin gets an error of about zero, and \(y_{\rm neb}\)’s came out 5 to 8 times too small. BRAIN writes NaN for them, and says so in the file. It writes formal errors for \(A_V\), \(Y_V\), \(\sigma_\star\), \(v_\star\), the light-weighted \(\langle\log t\rangle\) and \(\langle Z\rangle\), the gas kinematics, every line flux, and, in nespop, \(A_V^{\rm neb}\), H\(\alpha\)/H\(\beta\), \(T_e\), \(n_e\), \(Q(\mathrm H)\) and H\(\beta\) predicted and measured.

Only where the fit reaches its noise. Where \(\chi^2\) per degree of freedom exceeds 2, the misfit is the templates’, correlated from pixel to pixel, and no factor on a covariance describes it. On MaNGA 8626-12704 (60 spaxels, nespop), the formal and bootstrap errors agreed to within a factor of 0.9–1.5 at continuum S/N 3–8 (\(\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \approx 1.4\)); at S/N 8–20 and 20–90 (\(\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}\) 9 and 45) the formal errors were 1.5 to 6.5 times smaller for most quantities. Above \(\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 2\), BRAIN therefore writes no formal error at all.

The bootstrap (error_samples: N)#

The whole fit, every stage and every decision in it, is repeated \(N\) times on spectra made of the best model plus its residuals with a random sign on every pixel:

\[ f^{(b)}_i = M_i + \epsilon^{(b)}_i\,(f_i - M_i), \qquad \epsilon^{(b)}_i = \pm 1 , \]

the wild bootstrap (Davidson & Flachaire 2008, as used for pPXF by Kacharov et al. 2018). Each quantity’s error is half the range between the 16th and 84th percentiles of its \(N\) values. It sees what the formal errors cannot: a template joining or leaving, a parameter moving onto a bound, a spaxel switching between FC and NC, \(T_e\) measured in some resamples and not others, residuals larger than the errors. Forty to a hundred resamples are enough; the cost is \(N\) fits. For a datacube the resamples of every spaxel are fitted together, as one inner cube, by the same pipeline.

Both methods were tested on noise draws of mock spectra with known truth, built independently of BRAIN (BC03 populations, a PyNeb nebular continuum and lines integrated over the pixels): 60 draws of each of three spectra per mode, with 10–80 per cent of the light at 5500 Å from 2 Myr stars, \(A_V\) = 0.3–1.0 and S/N 10–30. On real spectra the bootstrap is the estimate to quote, with one caveat: it keeps the size of each pixel’s residual but not the correlation between pixels that template mismatch has, so where the mismatch is large its errors are an estimate rather than a calibration. No method can be checked against a truth that real data do not have.

Two limits remain, with either method. An age bin that is empty in the best fit gets too small an error even from the bootstrap, because resamples drawn around a model with no light there rarely put light there: read it as a lower limit. And the bootstrap’s line-based errors can be up to 1.45 times too small at moderate S/N, because a strong line is fitted by a few pixels whose residuals are smaller than their noise; prefer the formal errors for these, where they are written.

Degeneracies#

Age and metallicity, and age and dust, trade against each other: an older, metal-poorer population looks like a younger, metal-richer one, and a little more dust like a little more age. A complete treatment of these degeneracies is beyond the scope of this page (and, arguably, of any single page). In practice, individual \(x_j\) are poorly determined while averages over them are robust; report the averages, with their bootstrap errors.
