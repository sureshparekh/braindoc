Dust#

The attenuation#

Every stellar template, every featureless template and every emission line is attenuated by a screen of dust normalised at \(\lambda_0\):

\[ r_\lambda(A_V) = 10^{-0.4\,A_V\,[k(\lambda) - k(\lambda_0)]}, \qquad k(\lambda) = A_\lambda/A_V . \]

Normalised this way, \(A_V\) changes the continuum’s shape but not its value at \(\lambda_0\), so the weights stay light fractions at \(\lambda_0\). \(A_V\) is fitted within dust_range, \([-1, 5]\) mag by default.

Young populations. The templates flagged young in the library’s catalogue receive \(A_V + Y_V\), with \(Y_V\) fitted within young_extra_dust_range (\([0, 5]\) mag): young stars often sit inside their birth clouds, behind more dust than the older population. BasesGM flags none, so \(Y_V\) is exactly 0 with it.

The emission lines are attenuated by the stellar \(A_V\) in the model, so a line’s fitted weight is its flux before that dust: the summary reports line fluxes corrected for \(A_V\) relative to \(\lambda_0\), and line_fluxes() with observed=True gives them as they arrive.

The nebular continuum of nespop carries its own extinction, \(A_V^{\rm neb}\), not the stellar \(A_V\) (The nebular emission).

Why \(A_V\) may be negative#

The lower limit of \(-1\) is deliberate. Light reaching us through the galaxy itself, from its far side for example, has crossed the galaxy’s own dust; correcting for it would need a model of the geometry, which every group builds differently. It is common practice instead to let \(A_V\) go below zero, and a negative value also lets the fit say that the templates are bluer than the data need, rather than pinning \(A_V\) at zero and hiding the mismatch in the populations. Set dust_range: [0, 5] to forbid it.

The CCM law, with \(x = 1/\lambda\) in µm⁻¹, is \(k = a(x) + b(x)/R_V\), with, in the optical and near-infrared (\(1.1 \le x < 3.3\), \(y = x - 1.82\)),

\[ a = 1 + 0.17699y - 0.50447y^2 - 0.02427y^3 + 0.72085y^4 + 0.01979y^5 - 0.77530y^6 + 0.32999y^7, \]
\[ b = 1.41338y + 2.28305y^2 + 1.07233y^3 - 5.38434y^4 - 0.62251y^5 + 5.30260y^6 - 2.09002y^7, \]

\(a = 0.574x^{1.61}\) and \(b = -0.527x^{1.61}\) in the infrared (\(0.3 \le x < 1.1\)), and the ultraviolet branches of Cardelli et al. above \(x = 3.3\).

The curves differ mostly in the ultraviolet, so over 3700–6900 Å they give similar answers. On one MaNGA spectrum: \(A_V\) = 0.220 (CCM), 0.254 (CAL), 0.188 (GD1) and 0.211 (GD24), with \(\chi^2/N\) within 0.3 per cent of each other. brainsp.reddening_laws evaluates any of them.

Dust and age#

Age, metallicity and dust trade against one another in an optical spectrum: a little more dust makes a population look a little older, and an older, metal-poorer population looks like a younger, metal-richer one. This degeneracy, a long-standing and remarkably faithful companion of stellar population studies, is why BRAIN reports averages over templates and why the bootstrap is the uncertainty to quote for them (Uncertainties).

The Milky Way’s dust#

The Milky Way’s reddening is not fitted. It is removed before the fit, at the observed wavelengths, with \(A_\lambda = R_V\,E(B-V)\,k(\lambda)\) from the Galactic law, \(R_V\) and \(E(B-V)\) you give (Corrections before the fit).
