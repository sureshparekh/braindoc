Kinematics and the non-linear fit#

The parameters#

\[ \boldsymbol\theta = (A_V,\; Y_V,\; \sigma_\star,\; v_\star,\; \sigma_{g_1}, v_{g_1},\; \sigma_{g_2}, v_{g_2}, \dots) , \]

two per kinematic group of emission lines in the line modes. Each is bounded: dust_range, young_extra_dust_range, dispersion_range, velocity_range, gas_dispersion_range and gas_velocity_range.

Broadening in Fourier space#

On a grid uniform in \(\ln\lambda\), a Doppler shift is a translation: \(\lambda_{\rm obs} = \lambda(1+v/c)\) gives \(\ln\lambda_{\rm obs} \simeq \ln\lambda + v/c\), a shift of \(v/\Delta v\) cells at every wavelength, and a velocity distribution becomes a convolution kernel independent of wavelength. BRAIN builds this grid finer than the narrowest data pixel. For a Gaussian line-of-sight velocity distribution, each template is broadened and shifted at once in Fourier space,

\[ \tilde b_j(\omega) \;\longmapsto\; \tilde b_j(\omega)\, \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12\left(\frac{\omega\,\sigma_\star}{\Delta v}\right)^2 - i\,\frac{\omega\, v_\star}{\Delta v}\right], \]

with \(\omega\) in radians per cell. The transform of every template is computed once per fit, so a trial \((v_\star, \sigma_\star)\) costs one multiplication and one inverse transform. The templates are first extended at both ends, holding their end values, by more than the largest shift plus eight times the largest dispersion the bounds allow, so that the periodic transform never wraps one end of the spectrum onto the other.

The velocity search#

The non-linear fit needs a starting velocity in the right basin. \(\chi^2\) is evaluated on a grid of \(v_\star\) across velocity_range, in steps of velocity_grid_step (150 km s⁻¹), and the best point starts the fit. With coarse_to_fine_grid (default) the grid is scanned in two passes, coarse and then fine around the coarse minimum. velocity_range must contain the galaxy’s velocity relative to the frame the spectrum is in: for a spectrum still at redshift \(z\), roughly \(cz\).

Variable projection#

\(\chi^2\) is minimised over \(\boldsymbol\theta\) with the weights projected out: every evaluation at a trial \(\boldsymbol\theta\) solves the linear problem exactly (The linear solve), so the optimiser sees

\[ \boldsymbol\rho(\boldsymbol\theta) = W^{1/2}\big(\mathbf f - A(\boldsymbol\theta)\,\hat{\mathbf w}(\boldsymbol\theta)\big), \qquad \hat{\mathbf w}(\boldsymbol\theta) = \arg\min_{\mathbf w\ge0}\chi^2(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf w), \]

a residual of a handful of parameters instead of a hundred.

Projected Levenberg–Marquardt#

Each iteration solves

\[ \big(J^{\sf T}J + \mu\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf s)^2\big)\,\boldsymbol\delta = -J^{\sf T}\boldsymbol\rho , \]

with \(J = \partial\boldsymbol\rho/\partial\boldsymbol\theta\) and \(\mathbf s\) the running maximum of the columns’ norms (Moré’s scaling), and clips \(\boldsymbol\theta + \boldsymbol\delta\) into the bounds. A step is accepted if it lowers \(\chi^2\); the damping \(\mu\) falls by a factor of 3 after a good step (gain ratio above 0.75) and rises by 3 after a poor or rejected one. The fit stops when the relative change in \(\chi^2\) is below fit_tolerance and the step is below fit_tolerance of the bounds’ width (after at least four iterations), when \(\chi^2\) has stagnated over five accepted steps, after ten consecutive rejections, or at max_iterations (200). A spaxel that stops at max_iterations is reported as not converged.

The exact Jacobian#

\(J\) is computed exactly, by the Golub–Pereyra variable-projection formula. With \(P\) the passive set of the weights and \(G = A_P^{\sf T}A_P\),

\[ \frac{\partial\boldsymbol\rho}{\partial\theta_i} = -(I - \Pi_P)\,\frac{\partial A}{\partial\theta_i}\hat{\mathbf w} \;-\; A_P G^{-1}\Big[\Big(\frac{\partial A}{\partial\theta_i}\Big)^{\sf T}\boldsymbol\rho\Big]_P , \qquad \Pi_P = A_P G^{-1}A_P^{\sf T}, \]

with \(\partial A/\partial\theta_i\) from the analytic derivatives of the broadening kernel and of the attenuation. On 64 MaNGA spaxels it gives the fit finite differences give (the largest change in any fitted value \(5\times10^{-4}\) km s⁻¹), 2.4 to 3.9 times faster.

Observed and intrinsic dispersion#

The fitted \(\sigma_\star\) is the broadening applied to the library: the quadrature sum of the stars’ intrinsic dispersion and the difference between the data’s and the library’s line-spread functions. When both resolutions are known, BRAIN reports the intrinsic dispersion,

\[ \sigma_{\star,\rm intr} = \mathrm{sign}(x)\sqrt{|x|}, \qquad x = \sigma_{\rm obs}^2 - \Delta\sigma_{\rm inst}^2 , \]

and an upper limit when the fit sits on the lower bound of dispersion_range or the instrumental difference alone exceeds what the fit applied (Emission lines and resolution).
