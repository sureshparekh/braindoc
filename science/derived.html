Derived quantities#

Everything BRAIN reports beyond the fitted parameters is derived from the weights \(w_j\) of the \(N_\star\) stellar templates, of ages \(t_j\) and metallicities \(Z_j\).

Light fractions#

\[ x_j = 100\,\frac{w_j}{\sum_i w_i}\quad[\%], \]

summed over every component, the AGN templates included: the share of the attenuated model at \(\lambda_0\).

Masses#

A weight is a flux at \(\lambda_0\), and a template’s library flux per unit initial mass there is \(\phi_j\) (the (L/M)_j column of the summary). Undoing the attenuation the template suffered,

\[ M^{\rm ini}_j = \frac{w_j\,F_{\lambda_0}\,10^{\,0.4\,(A_V + Y_V\delta_j)\,k(\lambda_0)}}{\phi_j}, \qquad M^{\rm cor}_j = M^{\rm ini}_j\, m^\star_j , \]

with \(m^\star_j\) the fraction of the initial mass still in stars and remnants at \(t_j\) (MSTARS). The extinction factor at \(\lambda_0\) matters: the model’s attenuation is normalised there, so a weight is the template’s attenuated flux, and leaving the factor out would make the dustiest populations look the lightest. The mass fractions \(\mu_j = 100\,M_j/\sum_i M_i\) run over the stellar templates only, since an AGN component has light and no mass.

The totals Mini_tot and Mcor_tot are apparent: BRAIN uses no distance, so multiply them by the flux unit times \(4\pi d^2/L_\odot\) for solar masses. A datacube given galaxy_distance carries the masses in M☉ and the star formation rate maps.

Mean ages and metallicities#

\[ \langle\log t\rangle_L = \frac{\sum_j x_j\log_{10}t_j}{\sum_j x_j}, \qquad \langle\log t\rangle_M = \frac{\sum_j \mu_j\log_{10}t_j}{\sum_j \mu_j}, \]
\[ \langle Z\rangle_L = \frac{\sum_j x_j Z_j}{\sum_j x_j}, \qquad \langle Z\rangle_M = \frac{\sum_j \mu_j Z_j}{\sum_j \mu_j}, \]

over the stellar templates, with present mass in the mass-weighted ones. These are means of \(\log t\), not the logarithm of the mean age, and of \(Z\) as a mass fraction, not of [Z/H]. In a megacube (Mage_L, Mage_M, MZ_L, MZ_M), the light-weighted pair keeps the definition of the published MaNGA megacubes (Riffel et al. 2023): the numerator over the stellar templates, the denominator over every component, so that featureless light lowers them.

Age bins#

A megacube’s PoPBins holds the light and mass in broad age bins, each (name, youngest, oldest) in years, a template belonging to a bin when \(\text{youngest} < t_j \le \text{oldest}\). The default is the six MaNGA bins:

Star formation rates#

With sfr_bins and galaxy_distance, each bin (name, min_yr, max_yr) gives the mass formed in that age range divided by its width, in M☉ yr⁻¹: the star formation rate averaged over the bin.

Line fluxes and equivalent widths of a datacube#

Over \(\lambda_c \pm 5\sigma\) of each line,

\[ F_M = \int \ell(\lambda)\,d\lambda, \qquad EW_M = \int -\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{C(\lambda)}\,d\lambda, \qquad EW_D = \int\Big(1 - \frac{f(\lambda)}{C(\lambda)}\Big)\,d\lambda, \]

with \(\ell\) the line’s model profile (observed, attenuated), \(f\) the data and \(C\) a straight line fitted to the stellar model in the line’s continuum windows. FLUX_M and EQW_M are measured on the model, FLUX_D and EQW_D on the data; equivalent widths are negative for emission.
