A fitted spectrum#

When a spectrum’s fit finishes, the interface draws the model over the data and shows the headline numbers. Everything the fit measured is in the files it wrote.

On screen#

Where

What

upper panel

the total model in blue over the data, with the stellar part in orange and the emission lines in green

lower panel

the residuals, data minus model, on the same wavelengths

bottom bar

\(A_V\), the observed dispersion \(\sigma\), the stellar velocity \(v_0\), \(\chi^2/N\) and the mean absolute deviation (adev, per cent)

step 5

the summary of the run, and the folder the files went to

Zoom with the scroll wheel, or with the toolbar above the plot; double-click to see the whole range again. Fitting another spectrum replaces the plot; the files of every fit stay in brain_out.

The files#

In brain_out, named after the spectrum:

File

Content

<name>_summary.txt

every fitted and derived quantity, the population vector, the gas kinematics, the 1σ uncertainties and the emission-line table

<name>_fitted.txt

the observed and model spectra, column by column

<name>_settings.txt

every setting of the fit, to repeat it

<name>_fit.pdf

the standard figure, when Also save a PDF plot is ticked

Spectrum output files explains every line of these files.

Going further in Python#

The interface shows the headline numbers; the summary holds everything. To read it, plot the star formation history or compare fits, open it in Python:

import brainsp
r = brainsp.read_result("brain_out/mock_star_forming_lines_summary.txt")
r.av, r.av_err, r.mean_log_age_light, r.lines["Hbeta_4861"]
brainsp.plots.sfh(r, "sfh.png")

Reading a spectrum’s results lists everything a result holds.
