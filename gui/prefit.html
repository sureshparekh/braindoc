Preparing a datacube#

Before the fit, a datacube can be binned, degraded in resolution, filtered and dereddened. Each of these stages is optional and off until you tick it, and each writes its result to the megacube as a new extension, so the original data are always kept beside what was fitted.

The stages in the order they run. Each reads the extension the stage before it wrote, and the fit reads the last one.

In the interface the preparation stages are in step 3 Preparation, the Galactic dereddening in step 2 Corrections and the S/N masks in step 4 Masks; whatever step they are set in, they run in the order above. The same stages are available in Python and on the command line (Pre-fitting stages).

Spatial resampling#

Control

What it does

Spaxels per side

sums square blocks of n × n spaxels into one, on the flux and on the uncertainty together

It writes FLUX_RSP and IVAR_RSP (named after the extensions it read). The quality mask is dropped, since a flag cannot be summed. Binning raises the S/N per spaxel at the cost of spatial resolution; for a field with a strong gradient, prefer a S/N cut and fit the spaxels as they are.

Resolution#

Control

What it does

Data is

the data’s resolution, as R, FWHM (Å) or sigma (Å), with at reference λ when it varies with wavelength

Degrade to

the resolution to degrade to, in the same forms

It convolves the data down to a coarser resolution and writes <flux>_DEGR and <uncertainty>_DEGR.

Warning

This smooths the data. Neighbouring pixels become correlated, and every \(\chi^2\) in BRAIN assumes they are not, so the fit statistic and the uncertainties after it are optimistic by an amount nobody has quantified. BRAIN’s own route is the opposite one: the instrumental_resolution setting convolves the templates up to the data instead (Emission lines and resolution). Asking for both is refused before the run starts. Use this stage only to reproduce a processing someone else applied.

Spectral binning#

Control

What it does

Step [Å]

puts the cube on a uniform wavelength step, on the flux and the uncertainty together

It writes <flux>_BIN and <uncertainty>_BIN. The fit does not need it: BRAIN fits the data on their own pixels, whatever their spacing (Native pixels). Binning, like any resampling, correlates the noise, so use it only to reproduce someone else’s processing.

Butterworth filter#

Control

What it does

Order

the filter’s order, 3 by default

Range

its cut-off radius in the spatial-frequency plane, normalised to the largest radius; 0.3 by default

A low-pass filter over the two spatial axes only, nothing along the spectrum, with the transfer function \(H = 1 - 1/[1 + (\mathrm{range}/r)^{2\,\mathrm{order}}]\) on the normalised frequency radius \(r\), writing FLUX_BW. It mixes neighbouring spaxels, so the noise after it is correlated across the field: at the default order and range one spaxel’s light is spread over about six neighbours. Light that lands on a spaxel with no data is lost; the fraction of the flux that survived is measured, logged and written to the header keyword BW_KEPT. On a small field (under about 24 spaxels a side) the kernel covers most of the field, and the panel warns that the flux will come out well below what went in.

Dereddening#

Set in step 2 Corrections: the Milky Way’s extinction removed from the flux, with a Curve (any of BRAIN’s extinction laws), E(B−V) and R_V, writing FLUX_DRD. \(A_V = R_V\,E(B-V)\) as set here, and the \(R_V\) recorded in the header is the one applied.

Signal-to-noise masks#

Set in step 4 Masks (Fitting a datacube): one plane SN_MASKS_<t> for each threshold, and the S/N map SNMAP. The fit then takes the spaxels one of these masks keeps.
