Wavelength masks#

A wavelength mask leaves regions of the spectrum out of the fit, or makes them count more. Both workspaces have one: in the Spectrum workspace it is step 3 Masks; in the Datacube workspace it is the Wavelength mask panel of step 4 Masks, and it applies to every spaxel.

Draw a region#

  • Drag across the spectrum, or across the thin strip below it, to mark a region. In the Datacube workspace, click a spaxel on the map first, then drag across its spectrum.

  • Right-click inside a region to remove it.

  • Each new region takes the weight selected above the list:

Weight

Effect

exclude 0

the pixels are left out of the fit

normal 1

the pixels count as usual (Spectrum workspace only; useful to cut a hole in a wider region)

emphasise 2

the pixels count double

The regions are listed under the weights, with their limits and weight, and shaded on the plot.

The buttons#

Button

What it does

Load mask file…

reads a mask file: .sm or .gm (the number of regions on the first line, then start end weight per line) or a plain list of start end weight

Standard optical

adds the standard SDSS windows: sky residuals and the usual optical emission lines

Save mask…

writes the regions as a .sm file, to reuse in another fit, in Python or on the command line

Remove selected

removes the region selected in the list

Clear all

removes every region

Warning

Masks apply in every mode. The Standard optical mask masks the usual emission lines, which is what spop needs; when fitting lines (espop, nespop), do not mask the lines you want measured.

The same mask elsewhere#

A mask saved here is an ordinary file. Give it to the mask setting in Python or in a settings file, or with --mask to brainsp cube (Wavelength masks). The fitted wavelength range is not a mask: it is set in the Fitting range panel of the model (The model panel).
