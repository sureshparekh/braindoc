The model#

The fitted spectrum#

The observed flux \(F_\lambda\), with uncertainty \(\sigma_\lambda\), is resampled (flux-conservingly) onto a grid uniform in \(\ln\lambda\), with a velocity step \(\Delta v = c\,\Delta\ln\lambda\) set by the data’s sampling, and divided by its value at the normalisation wavelength \(\lambda_0\) (normalisation_wavelength, 5500 Å; the median over \(\pm 10\) pixels):

\[ f_\lambda = F_\lambda / F_{\lambda_0}, \qquad F_{\lambda_0} = \texttt{Flux\_tot}. \]

The model of \(f_\lambda\) is

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} M_\lambda = {} & \sum_{j=1}^{N_\star} w_j\, \big[b_j \ast G(v_\star, \sigma_\star)\big]_\lambda\, r_\lambda(A_V + Y_j) \\ & + \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\rm gas}} w_{N_\star+k}\, g_{k,\lambda}(v_{g(k)}, \sigma_{g(k)})\, r_\lambda(A_V) \;+\; w_{\rm neb}\, n_\lambda , \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

where

  • \(b_j\) is template \(j\) (an SSP, or an AGN continuum), normalised to 1 at \(\lambda_0\), resampled onto the fitting grid (and, with instrumental_resolution, first broadened to the data’s line-spread function; Instrumental resolution);

  • \(G(v, \sigma)\) is a Gaussian line-of-sight velocity distribution and \(\ast\) convolution in \(\ln\lambda\) (Kinematics);

  • \(r_\lambda(A) = 10^{-0.4 A [q(\lambda) - q(\lambda_0)]}\) is the dust attenuation, normalised at \(\lambda_0\), \(q = A_\lambda/A_V\) the extinction law (Dust); \(Y_j\) is extra extinction for templates flagged young (YAV), 0 otherwise;

  • \(g_k\) is an area-normalised emission-line profile (or a tied doublet’s), with the velocity and dispersion of its kinematic group \(g(k)\) (Emission lines);

  • \(n_\lambda\) is the nebular continuum, in nepop (Nebular physics);

  • \(w \ge 0\) are the linear weights.

Because every \(b_j\) is 1 at \(\lambda_0\) and \(r_\lambda(\cdot)\) is 1 there, \(w_j\) is template \(j\)’s contribution to the (attenuated) model at \(\lambda_0\), so the light fractions are \(x_j = 100\, w_j / \sum_{i \le N_\star} w_i\) per cent.

The objective#

\[ \chi^2(\theta, w) = \sum_\lambda m_\lambda \left[\frac{f_\lambda - M_\lambda(\theta, w)}{\sigma_\lambda / F_{\lambda_0}}\right]^2 + \lVert D\, w_\star \rVert^2 \;(+\; \text{penalty rows in } \texttt{photopop}) \]

with \(m_\lambda\) the mask weight (0 excluded, 1 normal, >1 up-weighted; Masks and weights), \(D\) a regularisation operator on the stellar weights (The linear solve), and \(\theta = (A_V, Y_V, \sigma_\star, v_\star, \sigma_{g_1}, v_{g_1}, \ldots)\) the non-linear parameters. BRAIN minimises it over \(w \ge 0\) exactly for each \(\theta\) (The linear solve) and over \(\theta\) iteratively (The non-linear fit):

\[ \hat\theta = \arg\min_{\theta \in [\theta_{\rm lo}, \theta_{\rm hi}]} \; \min_{w \ge 0} \chi^2(\theta, w). \]

Outlier rejection#

With reject_outliers (default on), after the fit every pixel whose residual exceeds outlier_threshold (3) times its error is given zero weight and the fit is run again: one clip-and-refit pass, as STARLIGHT’s NSIGMA clipping. With outlier_scale: typical the threshold multiplies the larger of the error and the spectrum’s typical residual instead, so a model that cannot match a very high-S/N spectrum anywhere does not lose most of its pixels. \(N_{\rm eff}\), \(\chi^2\) and the mean deviation are then over the pixels left.
